The best Christmas dinner ideas start with the size of your table. For 2 to 4 people, pick a quick main like roast chicken or salmon with two sides. For 6 to 12, choose one big roast, such as ham or prime rib, and lean on make-ahead sides. Then add one dessert you can bake the day before.

Below are ready-made menus for every group size, a list of mains, sides and desserts to mix and match, and a simple timeline so it all comes out hot.

## What are good Christmas dinner menus by table size?

Each menu is built around one main, two or three sides and a dessert. Swap freely.

### Christmas dinner for two

- **Main:** pan-seared steaks or roast Cornish hens
- **Sides:** garlic mashed potatoes and roasted green beans
- **Dessert:** a small batch of brownies or two individual lava cakes

Dinner for two is the moment to splurge on a better cut, because you only need a little.

### Christmas dinner for 4 to 6

- **Main:** a whole roast chicken or a pork tenderloin
- **Sides:** roasted root vegetables, a green salad and dinner rolls
- **Dessert:** an apple crumble or a pan of Christmas cookies

### Christmas dinner for 8 to 12

- **Main:** a glazed spiral ham or a prime rib roast
- **Sides:** a cheesy potato bake, roasted Brussels sprouts, a green bean dish and rolls
- **Dessert:** two pies, or a pie plus a tray of cookies

For big groups, choose sides that bake in one dish and hold well. Casseroles are your friend.

### A vegetarian Christmas table

- **Main:** mushroom wellington or stuffed acorn squash
- **Sides:** creamy polenta, maple roasted carrots and a winter salad
- **Dessert:** gingerbread cake

## Which Christmas dinner mains are easiest to get right?

These are described in general terms. Pick one based on your oven and your crowd.

1. **Glazed ham.** Most are already cooked, so you are mostly heating and glazing. Very forgiving.
2. **Prime rib.** Impressive and simple, but use a thermometer. Pull it a few degrees below your target, since it keeps cooking as it rests.
3. **Beef tenderloin.** Cooks fast and slices beautifully for a crowd.
4. **Roast turkey or turkey breast.** A breast roasts much faster than a whole bird. Cook to 165°F (74°C).
5. **Roast chicken.** The easy choice for small tables. Also 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part.
6. **Pork loin or tenderloin.** Cook to 145°F (63°C) and rest for 3 minutes.
7. **Baked salmon.** Ready in about 15 minutes. Good for a lighter Christmas Eve, and we have more ideas for [what to make for dinner on Christmas Eve](../what-to-make-for-dinner-christmas-eve/).
8. **Mushroom wellington.** A meat-free centerpiece that still feels like an occasion.

## What sides go with Christmas dinner?

Pick one starch, one or two vegetables and bread. That covers almost every plate.

### Potatoes and starches

- Garlic mashed potatoes
- Scalloped or cheesy potato bake
- Crispy roast potatoes
- Mac and cheese
- Wild rice pilaf

### Vegetables

- Roasted Brussels sprouts with bacon or balsamic
- Green beans with toasted almonds
- Honey or maple glazed carrots
- Creamed spinach
- A crisp winter salad with pears and nuts

### Bread

- Soft dinner rolls
- Garlic bread for a big crowd

Many of the [holiday casserole](../thanksgiving-recipes-casserole/) sides you made for Thanksgiving work just as well at Christmas.

## What desserts work for Christmas dinner?

Choose one that you can make ahead, so dessert is not competing with the roast for oven space.

- Christmas cookies, decorated or plain. Our [easy Christmas cookies](../christmas-cookies-easy/) guide has beginner-friendly picks.
- Pecan, apple or chocolate cream pie
- Gingerbread cake
- Yule log
- Cheesecake, which actually tastes better made a day ahead
- Bread pudding

## How do you time Christmas dinner so everything is hot?

The trick is to work backward from when you want to eat.

### 2 to 3 days before

- Shop and clear fridge space.
- Make cranberry sauce, salad dressing and dessert.

### The day before

- Assemble casseroles and refrigerate them unbaked.
- Peel and cut vegetables. Keep potatoes covered in cold water in the fridge.
- Set the table.

### On the day

- Take the roast out of the fridge for a short while before cooking, as your recipe directs.
- Roast the main. While it rests, bake the casseroles and roast vegetables.
- Warm the rolls last.

A rested roast stays hot for a while under loose foil, which gives you the oven window you need for sides.

### Food safety while you host

Do not leave cooked food out at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Refrigerate leftovers in shallow containers so they cool quickly, and eat them within 3 to 4 days.
