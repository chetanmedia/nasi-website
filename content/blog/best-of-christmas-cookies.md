The best Christmas cookies balance three things: flavor, how hard they are to make, and how well they keep. Our top picks are cut-out sugar cookies, gingerbread people, chocolate crinkles, shortbread and thumbprints, with a few no-bake treats to fill out the tray. Below is our full ranking of 25 holiday cookies, grouped so you can plan by time and mood.

We ranked them on taste, effort and shelf life, not on any single recipe. Every family has its own version, so use this as a menu and grab a recipe you trust for each.

## How did we rank these Christmas cookies?

Each cookie got a quick score on three questions:

- **Flavor:** does it taste like the holidays, and do people reach for a second one?
- **Effort:** can a home baker make a batch in an afternoon?
- **Keeping:** will it still taste good on day five, or in a gift tin?

A cookie that is fussy but unforgettable can still rank high. A cookie that goes stale by tomorrow drops a few spots.

## What are the best Christmas cookies overall?

These are the ten that earn a spot on almost every holiday tray.

1. **Cut-out sugar cookies.** Buttery, sturdy and made for icing. The centerpiece of any tray.
2. **Gingerbread people.** Warm spice, a little chew, and the most fun to decorate with kids.
3. **Chocolate crinkles.** Fudgy middles under a cracked powdered sugar coat. Looks fancy, takes little skill.
4. **Shortbread.** Three ingredients, rich and crumbly, and it keeps for ages.
5. **Jam thumbprints.** A buttery cookie with a jewel of red or golden jam in the middle.
6. **Snickerdoodles.** Soft, tangy and rolled in cinnamon sugar. Quick and loved by everyone.
7. **Peanut butter blossoms.** A soft peanut butter cookie topped with a chocolate kiss.
8. **Russian tea cakes (snowballs).** Nutty little domes rolled twice in powdered sugar.
9. **Gingersnaps.** Crisp, spicy and great for dunking in coffee.
10. **Spritz cookies.** Pressed butter cookies in wreaths and trees, a classic for big batches.

## Which Christmas cookies are quickest to make?

Short on time? These come together fast with no chilling or rolling.

11. **Chocolate chip with holiday sprinkles.** Your usual favorite, dressed for December.
12. **No-bake oat cookies.** Chocolate, peanut butter and oats, set on the counter.
13. **Melted chocolate pretzel bites.** Salty, sweet, and kids can make them.
14. **Cake mix cookies.** A box mix plus eggs and oil, baked into soft rounds.
15. **Rum or bourbon balls.** No oven needed, and they get better after a day or two.

## Which cookies are worth the extra effort?

When you have a slow weekend and want a showstopper:

16. **Linzer cookies.** Two delicate nut cookies sandwiched with jam, with a window cut in the top.
17. **Stained glass cookies.** Crushed hard candy melts into the middle for a see-through center.
18. **Pizzelle.** Thin, crisp anise wafers made on a special iron.
19. **Rugelach.** Cream cheese dough rolled around jam, nuts and cinnamon.
20. **Decorated royal icing cookies.** The full flood-and-pipe treatment for gifting.

## Which cookies are best for gifting and shipping?

Sturdy cookies travel well and stay fresh in a tin.

21. **Biscotti.** Twice-baked and crunchy. One of the best keepers there is.
22. **Chocolate-dipped shortbread.** Add a coat of chocolate for a gift-shop look.
23. **Molasses cookies.** Soft and chewy, and they stay that way for days.
24. **Brown butter pecan sandies.** Nutty, crumbly and not too sweet.
25. **Peppermint bark.** Not a cookie, but always welcome in a holiday box.

Pack crisp and soft cookies in separate tins. Soft cookies give off moisture and can turn crisp ones limp.

## How do you build a balanced Christmas cookie tray?

A great tray mixes colors, shapes and textures. Try this simple formula:

| Slot | Pick one | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Buttery | Shortbread or spritz | A calm, rich base |
| Chocolate | Crinkles or blossoms | Everyone's first grab |
| Spiced | Gingerbread or gingersnaps | Tastes like the season |
| Fruity | Thumbprints or Linzers | Adds color |
| Fun | Decorated sugar cookies | The showpiece |
| No-bake | Pretzel bites or rum balls | Easy filler |

## How do you plan your holiday baking without stress?

Holiday baking goes smoothly when you spread it out.

### Make dough ahead

Most cookie doughs freeze well for up to three months. Wrap tightly, label with the name and date, and thaw in the fridge overnight before baking.

### Bake in an order that makes sense

Bake the best keepers first, like biscotti, shortbread and gingersnaps. Save soft and frosted cookies for the last few days before you serve or give them.

### Schedule your bake days

Put baking sessions on your calendar the way you would a meal. Our guide on [how to meal plan for a week](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/) works just as well for a cookie week.

### Bake safely

Do not eat raw dough that contains raw flour or raw eggs. Cool cookies fully before stacking or storing so they do not get soggy.
