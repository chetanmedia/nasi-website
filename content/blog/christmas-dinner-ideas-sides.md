The best Christmas dinner ideas for sides are a mix of one rich starch, one or two roasted or green vegetables, and one fresh, sharp dish to cut through it all. Pick at least two you can make ahead, so the oven is free for the main on the day. Below are 27 side ideas grouped by how and when you cook them, plus portions and a simple plan.

If you are still choosing the main event, start with our [Christmas dinner ideas](../christmas-dinner-ideas/) for full menus by table size, then come back here to fill in the plate.

## How do you choose sides for Christmas dinner?

Think about balance first, then about your oven. Most holiday plates go wrong in one of two ways: everything is beige and creamy, or every dish needs the oven at a different temperature at 5 PM.

### Balance the plate

A good Christmas spread usually has:

- **One starch** people will fight over, like potatoes, stuffing or a gratin.
- **One or two vegetables**, ideally one roasted and one green.
- **Something bright**, such as a crunchy salad, a pickle or a cranberry relish.
- **Bread**, whether that is rolls, biscuits or Yorkshire puddings.

If your main is rich, like prime rib or a glazed ham, lean harder on the bright and green side of the list.

### Plan around your oven

Write down every dish and the oven temperature it needs. Anything that can be made on the stovetop, in a slow cooker, or served at room temperature takes pressure off. Most roasts need to rest for 20 to 40 minutes before carving, and that resting window is when you can roast vegetables or reheat a make-ahead casserole at high heat.

## What are the best make-ahead Christmas sides?

These are the sides that let you enjoy the day. Most hold well for 1 to 2 days in the fridge. Cool them quickly, keep them covered, and reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

### Make 1 to 2 days ahead

1. **Classic mashed potatoes.** Make them extra buttery, then reheat in a slow cooker on low with a splash of warm milk.
2. **Potato gratin.** Thin-sliced potatoes baked in cream and garlic. It slices more neatly the day after baking.
3. **Braised red cabbage.** Cabbage cooked slowly with apple and vinegar. It tastes better on day two.
4. **Cranberry sauce or relish.** Cooked or raw, it keeps well and adds the sharp note every plate needs.
5. **Sausage or herb stuffing.** Assemble the day before, then bake on the day so the top crisps.
6. **Sweet potato casserole.** Mash and spread the base ahead, then add the topping just before baking.

### Freeze weeks ahead

7. **Dinner rolls.** Bake, cool and freeze, then warm in a low oven wrapped in foil.
8. **Yorkshire pudding batter base.** Not the batter itself, but you can measure and store the dry mix ahead to save a step.
9. **Compound butter.** Herb or garlic butter rolled into a log, ready to melt over vegetables or bread.

## Which Christmas sides go in the oven?

Roasted sides add color and caramelized flavor. Group them by temperature so they can share the oven.

### High-heat roasted vegetables

10. **Crispy roast potatoes.** Parboil, rough up the edges, then roast in hot fat until deep golden.
11. **Honey-glazed carrots.** Roasted with a little honey, butter and thyme.
12. **Roasted Brussels sprouts.** Halved, roasted until charred, and finished with something sharp like lemon or balsamic. Our guide to [Thanksgiving Brussels sprout recipes](../thanksgiving-recipes-brussel-sprouts/) has more ways to dress them.
13. **Parsnips and carrots.** A traditional pairing, roasted together until the edges catch.
14. **Whole roasted squash wedges.** Butternut or delicata, with a spice rub and a drizzle of yogurt or tahini.

### Bakes and casseroles

15. **Green bean casserole.** The creamy, crunchy classic. See our [Thanksgiving green bean recipes](../thanksgiving-recipes-green-beans/) for fresher versions too.
16. **Baked mac and cheese.** A crowd favorite, especially if kids are at the table.
17. **Cauliflower cheese.** Florets baked in a cheese sauce until bubbling, a lighter swap for mac and cheese.
18. **Scalloped corn.** A soft, spoonable corn bake that goes well with ham.

## What Christmas sides can I make on the stovetop?

Stovetop sides cook while the oven is busy and often come together in under 20 minutes.

### Quick greens

19. **Garlicky sautéed green beans.** Blanch ahead, then toss in a hot pan with garlic and butter just before serving.
20. **Creamed spinach.** Rich and simple, and a natural partner for prime rib or beef tenderloin.
21. **Buttered peas with mint.** Frozen peas work perfectly here and take five minutes.

### Grains and extras

22. **Wild rice pilaf.** Cooked with mushrooms, herbs and dried cranberries. It holds well in a covered pot.
23. **Gravy.** Make a base ahead with stock and roux, then whisk in pan drippings once the roast is out.

## What are easy no-cook Christmas sides?

Every table needs one or two cold dishes. They add crunch and color and cost you no oven space.

24. **Winter citrus salad.** Sliced oranges, pomegranate seeds and a little red onion with a simple dressing.
25. **Kale or shaved Brussels sprout salad.** Tougher greens hold up well if you dress them an hour ahead.
26. **Burrata with roasted tomatoes or pesto.** Easy, festive and good as a starter or a side.
27. **Pickled red onions or a relish tray.** Quick pickles cut through rich roasts beautifully.

## How much of each side do you need?

Use this as a starting point. Adjust for your crowd and whether you want leftovers.

| Side type | Per person | For 8 guests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potatoes (mashed or roast) | 1/2 to 3/4 cup | 4 to 6 cups, about 3 to 4 lb raw |
| Vegetable side | 1/2 cup | About 4 cups |
| Stuffing | 1/2 to 3/4 cup | 4 to 6 cups |
| Salad | 1 cup loose greens | About 8 cups |
| Rolls | 1 to 2 | 12 to 16 |
| Gravy | 1/4 to 1/3 cup | 2 to 3 cups |

When you serve more than four sides, you can trim each portion a little, because people take smaller spoonfuls of more dishes. Potatoes are the exception. Always make extra.

## What does a sample Christmas sides plan look like?

Here is one way to spread the work for a dinner at 5 PM.

### Two days before

- Make cranberry sauce and braised red cabbage.
- Bake and freeze rolls if you have not already.
- Wash and dry salad greens, then store wrapped in paper towels.

### The day before

- Assemble the gratin and the stuffing. Cover and chill.
- Blanch green beans and chill them in a container.
- Peel and cut potatoes for roasting, and keep them in cold water in the fridge.

### On the day

- Mid-afternoon: make mashed potatoes and move them to a slow cooker on low.
- When the roast comes out to rest: turn the oven up, roast potatoes and vegetables, and reheat the gratin and stuffing.
- Final 10 minutes: sauté the green beans, finish the gravy, dress the salad and warm the rolls.

Food safety matters with a big spread. Do not leave cooked sides at room temperature for more than 2 hours, and get leftovers into the fridge promptly.
