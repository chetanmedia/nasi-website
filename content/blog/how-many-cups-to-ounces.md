There are 8 fluid ounces in 1 US cup, which is about 240 ml. That rule is exact for liquids like water, milk and stock. For dry ingredients, a cup still holds 8 fluid ounces of space, but the weight in ounces changes with the ingredient, so a cup of flour weighs about 4.25 ounces while a cup of sugar weighs about 7.

## How many ounces are in a cup of liquid?

For liquids, use the simple rule: **1 cup = 8 fluid ounces**. Measure in a clear liquid measuring cup set on the counter, and read it at eye level.

|---|---|---|
| 1/8 cup | 1 fl oz | 30 ml |
| 1/4 cup | 2 fl oz | 60 ml |
| 1/3 cup | 2.7 fl oz | 80 ml |
| 1/2 cup | 4 fl oz | 120 ml |
| 2/3 cup | 5.3 fl oz | 160 ml |
| 3/4 cup | 6 fl oz | 180 ml |
| 1 cup | 8 fl oz | 240 ml |
| 1 1/2 cups | 12 fl oz | 355 ml |

### Ounces to cups, the other way around

Divide fluid ounces by 8. So 12 fl oz is 1 1/2 cups, 20 fl oz is 2 1/2 cups, and a 32 fl oz carton of broth is 4 cups.

## How many ounces are in a cup of a dry ingredient?

Here is where people get tripped up. A dry measuring cup holds the same space as a liquid cup, but dry ingredients weigh different amounts. If a recipe lists ounces for flour, sugar or chocolate, it almost always means weight, so use a kitchen scale.

### Dry cups to ounces chart (weight per 1 cup)

| Ingredient (1 cup) | Ounces by weight | Grams |
|---|---|---|
| All-purpose flour, spooned and leveled | 4.25 oz | 120 g |
| Granulated sugar | 7 oz | 200 g |
| Brown sugar, packed | 7.5 oz | 213 g |
| Powdered sugar | 4 oz | 113 g |
| Butter (2 sticks) | 8 oz | 227 g |
| Rolled oats | 3.1 oz | 89 g |
| Chocolate chips | 6 oz | 170 g |
| Honey | 12 oz | 340 g |
| Water or milk | about 8.3 oz | about 236 g |

These are standard baking weights. Brands and how you fill the cup can shift them a little, which is exactly why bakers like scales.

### Why does flour weigh less than sugar?

Flour is light and full of air. Sugar crystals pack tightly. Same cup, different weight. If you scoop flour straight from the bag, you can pack in 20 percent more than the recipe expects, which gives you dry cakes and tough cookies. Spoon it into the cup and level it with a knife instead.

## What is the difference between fluid ounces and ounces?

Fluid ounces (fl oz) measure **volume**: how much space something takes up. Ounces (oz) measure **weight**: how heavy it is.

### When they match and when they don't

- **Water, milk, juice, stock:** 1 cup is 8 fl oz and weighs about 8 oz. Close enough to treat as the same.
- **Oil:** 8 fl oz weighs a bit less than 8 oz, because oil is lighter than water.
- **Honey, syrup, molasses:** 8 fl oz weighs about 11 to 12 oz, because they are dense.
- **Flour, cocoa, oats:** 1 cup weighs far less than 8 oz.

### How to tell which one a recipe means

If the recipe says "fl oz," measure volume. If it says "oz" next to a liquid like milk, volume is usually fine. If it says "oz" next to a dry ingredient, weigh it.

## How do US cups compare to other cups?

Most recipes online use US cups. A few older or international recipes use other sizes.

| Cup type | Volume |
|---|---|
| US customary cup | 8 US fl oz (236.6 ml) |
| US legal cup (nutrition labels) | 240 ml |
| Metric cup (Australia, Canada, NZ) | 250 ml |
| Imperial cup (older UK recipes) | 10 imperial fl oz (284 ml) |

For everyday cooking, the difference between 237 ml and 250 ml rarely matters. For baking, use the cup the recipe was written for, or switch to grams.

## How do you measure cups and ounces accurately?

### For liquids

1. Use a clear cup with a spout and marked lines.
2. Set it on a flat counter.
3. Pour, then crouch so your eyes are level with the line.

### For dry ingredients

1. Use nesting dry cups.
2. Spoon the ingredient in until it mounds.
3. Sweep the top flat with the back of a knife.
4. Pack only brown sugar, and only when the recipe says "packed."

### For small amounts

Under 1/4 cup, switch to spoons. There are 16 tablespoons in a cup and 2 tablespoons in a fluid ounce. Our guides to [how many tablespoons are in a cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-a-cup/) and [teaspoons to tablespoons](../teaspoon-is-how-many-tablespoons/) cover the spoon math.
