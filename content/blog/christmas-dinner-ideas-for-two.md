Christmas dinner for two works best when you cook one special main sized for two, add two good sides, and finish with an easy dessert. Think steaks, a small roast chicken, Cornish hens or salmon instead of a 14-pound turkey. You get all the feeling of a holiday meal with a fraction of the work and only a night or two of leftovers.

Below are 24 Christmas dinner ideas for two, grouped by mood and time, plus a simple plan for the day and tips for shrinking big holiday recipes down to size.

## Why is Christmas dinner for two different?

Most holiday recipes assume a crowd. A whole turkey, a 9x13 casserole and a full pie are built for eight or more. Cooking those for two means days of the same leftovers and a lot of time at the stove.

A smaller dinner gives you room to choose. You can splurge on one nice cut of meat. You can cook something you both love instead of what everyone expects. And you can actually sit down and enjoy it while it is hot.

### What makes a small Christmas dinner feel special?

It is rarely the size of the roast. It is the small touches:

- One main that feels like an occasion.
- A sauce or pan gravy, even a quick one.
- Candles, cloth napkins and real plates.
- A dessert you would not make on a Tuesday.
- No rush. Cook together, eat slowly.

## What are the best Christmas dinner mains for two?

Choose one of these and build the meal around it. Each is sized for two or easy to buy in a two-person portion.

### Classic and cozy (60 to 90 minutes)

1. **Roast chicken.** A small 3 to 4 pound bird gives you a full roast dinner and one round of leftovers.
2. **Cornish hens.** One per person feels fancy and roasts in about an hour.
3. **Bone-in turkey breast.** All the turkey flavor without the whole bird.
4. **Pork tenderloin with a fruit glaze.** Cranberry, apple or apricot all work.
5. **Small glazed ham steak or half-pound ham.** Sweet, salty and done quickly.
6. **Braised short ribs.** Mostly hands-off, and they taste even better made a day ahead.

### Elegant and date-night (under 45 minutes)

7. **Pan-seared steaks with butter and herbs.** Ribeye, strip or filet. Rest them before slicing.
8. **Salmon fillets with a citrus or herb crust.** Bright and light after a week of rich food.
9. **Seared scallops over something creamy.** Risotto, polenta or a cauliflower purée.
10. **Lamb chops with garlic and rosemary.** Quick to cook and very festive.
11. **Shrimp scampi with linguine.** A comforting bowl with a bit of sparkle.
12. **Duck breast with a cherry or orange sauce.** A restaurant feel at home.

### Vegetarian and plant-based

13. **Mushroom and chestnut Wellington.** A golden centerpiece that slices nicely for two.
14. **Stuffed acorn squash.** Halve one squash and fill it with grains, nuts and dried fruit.
15. **Baked brie or burrata with roasted vegetables and good bread.** A relaxed, shareable main.
16. **Creamy mushroom risotto.** Worth the stirring when there are only two portions.
17. **Paneer or tofu in a rich tomato and spice sauce.** Warm and filling, with rice or flatbread.

For food safety, use a thermometer: cook poultry to 165°F (74°C), pork, lamb and beef steaks to at least 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest, and fish to 145°F (63°C) or until it flakes easily.

## Which sides go with a small Christmas dinner?

Two sides is plenty. Pick one starchy and one green or bright.

18. **Smashed or roasted potatoes.** Crispy edges, fluffy middles, little effort.
19. **A small pan of stuffing.** Bake it in a loaf pan or a small baking dish.
20. **Honey roasted carrots or Brussels sprouts.** Sweet, caramelized and easy to share an oven with the main.
21. **Green beans with garlic and lemon.** Ready in 10 minutes on the stove.
22. **A winter salad.** Bitter greens, pear or orange, nuts and a sharp dressing to cut the richness.

## What is an easy Christmas dessert for two?

Skip the full-size cake. These are made for small portions.

23. **Individual chocolate lava cakes or mug cakes.** Rich, warm and done in minutes.
24. **A few decorated cookies and hot chocolate.** Bake a half batch, or freeze the rest of the dough. Our [easy Christmas cookies](../christmas-cookies-easy/) guide has beginner-friendly picks.

Other easy finishes: a small cheese plate with grapes and honey, poached pears, or store-bought ice cream with a quick warm sauce.

## How do I plan Christmas dinner for two without stress?

A simple timeline keeps the day calm. Here is one way to do it.

### One or two days before

- Shop for everything, and buy the main from the butcher counter in the exact size you need.
- Make the dessert if it keeps well.
- Wash and chop vegetables and store them in the fridge.
- Mix any dressing, glaze or sauce base.

### Christmas morning

- Take the main out of the fridge 20 to 30 minutes before cooking if it is a steak or chops. Larger roasts can go straight in.
- Set the table early. It makes the day feel like an event.

### About 90 minutes before dinner

- Start the roast or braise, or the potatoes if your main is quick.
- Put the second side in when there is about 30 minutes to go.
- Cook quick mains like steak, fish or scallops in the last 15 minutes.
- Let meat rest while you finish the sauce and warm the plates.

## How do I scale big holiday recipes down for two?

Many family favorites are written for eight or twelve. A few rules make halving or quartering them work.

### Do the math once

Divide each ingredient by the original servings, then multiply by two. A recipe for 8 becomes one quarter. Write the new amounts down before you start so you are not doing fractions with floury hands.

### Use a smaller pan

A half-size casserole in a full-size dish spreads thin and dries out. Use a loaf pan, an 8x8 pan or a small cast-iron skillet.

### Watch the clock, not the recipe

Smaller amounts cook faster. Start checking 10 to 15 minutes early, and trust a thermometer for meat.

### Be careful with eggs, leavening and salt

If the math gives you half an egg, beat one egg and use half by weight or eye. Add salt and strong spices a little at a time and taste at the end.

## What if we still end up with leftovers?

Even a small roast leaves a little extra. That is a bonus, not a problem. Chicken or turkey makes a great next-day sandwich, soup or pot pie. Cool leftovers and refrigerate them within 2 hours, and eat them within 3 to 4 days, or freeze them.
