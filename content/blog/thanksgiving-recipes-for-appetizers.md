The best Thanksgiving recipes for appetizers are small, light and mostly make-ahead: a dip with crisp vegetables, a cheese board, spiced nuts, deviled eggs and one warm bite. They keep guests happy while the turkey rests, without filling anyone up before the main event. Below are 26 ideas sorted by how much time they need, plus how much to make and a simple plan for the day.

## What makes a good Thanksgiving appetizer?

Thanksgiving starters have a different job than party food. Dinner is the star, so appetizers should take the edge off hunger, not compete with the meal.

### Keep it light

Aim for crunch and brightness: raw vegetables, pickles, fruit, a sharp cheese. Save the heavy, creamy, fried things for another party.

### Keep it out of the oven

The oven is busy with the turkey and the casseroles. The best Thanksgiving appetizers need no oven at all, or can be warmed in a slow cooker or on the stove. If oven space is your biggest worry, our [crockpot Thanksgiving recipes](../thanksgiving-recipes-in-crockpot/) help too.

### Make it ahead

Anything you can make the day before is one less thing to do while the bird roasts.

## How many Thanksgiving appetizers do you need?

Fewer than you think. Here is a quick guide.

| Guests | Number of appetizers | Bites per person |
|---|---|---|
| 4 to 6 | 2 to 3 | 3 to 4 |
| 8 to 12 | 3 to 4 | 4 to 5 |
| 15 or more | 4 to 5 | 4 to 5 |

If guests arrive more than two hours before dinner, add one more option or bump up the bites. If dinner is right after arrival, a single board is enough.

## What Thanksgiving appetizers take no cooking at all?

These come together in 15 minutes or less. Perfect for the host who has enough going on.

### No-cook ideas

1. **Fall cheese board.** Two or three cheeses, sliced apples and pears, grapes, nuts and crackers.
2. **Crudité platter with a dip.** Carrots, celery, radishes, snap peas and endive with ranch, hummus or a herby yogurt dip.
3. **Marinated olives.** Warm olives with citrus zest, garlic and herbs, or just toss them cold.
4. **Pickle plate.** Cornichons, pickled beets, pickled onions and dilly beans.
5. **Prosciutto-wrapped pears or melon.** Sweet and salty, ready in minutes.
6. **Caprese skewers.** Cherry tomatoes, mini mozzarella and basil on toothpicks.
7. **Apple slices with honey and blue cheese.** Toss apples in a little lemon juice so they stay pale.
8. **Cranberry salsa with chips.** Chopped fresh cranberries, jalapeño, cilantro, lime and a little sugar.

## Which Thanksgiving appetizers can you make the day before?

These are the host's best friends. Make them Wednesday, chill them, and set them out on Thursday.

### Make-ahead ideas

9. **Spiced nuts.** Pecans or mixed nuts roasted with butter, maple, cinnamon and a pinch of cayenne.
10. **Deviled eggs.** Fill them on the day, but cook and halve the eggs the day before.
11. **Pumpkin hummus.** Chickpeas blended with pumpkin purée, tahini, lemon and warm spices.
12. **Cheese ball.** Cream cheese and cheddar rolled in chopped pecans and herbs.
13. **Whipped feta.** Feta blended with Greek yogurt, topped with honey and pepper.
14. **Spinach artichoke dip.** Mix it ahead, then warm it in a small slow cooker.
15. **Roasted beet dip.** Bright pink, earthy and a nice change from the usual.
16. **Pimento cheese.** A Southern favorite, great with celery and crackers.
17. **Antipasto skewers.** Salami, cheese cubes, olives and marinated artichokes.

## What are some warm Thanksgiving appetizers?

One warm option makes the spread feel special. Pick one, not three.

### Warm ideas (20 to 40 minutes)

18. **Baked brie with cranberry.** Brie topped with cranberry sauce and pecans, warmed until soft.
19. **Stuffed mushrooms.** Mushroom caps filled with sausage or breadcrumbs, herbs and cheese.
20. **Butternut squash soup shots.** Small cups of smooth, spiced soup.
21. **Mini meatballs.** Kept warm in a slow cooker with a cranberry glaze.
22. **Sweet potato rounds.** Roasted slices topped with goat cheese and a bit of pecan.
23. **Bacon-wrapped dates.** Sweet, salty and gone in minutes.
24. **Crostini.** Toasted baguette slices with whipped ricotta, roasted squash or mushrooms. Toast ahead, top just before serving.
25. **Warm crab dip.** A rich, creamy option if seafood suits your crowd.
26. **Pigs in a blanket.** The kids' table will thank you.

If your oven is full, warm dips and meatballs in a slow cooker on low, and bake anything small in a toaster oven or air fryer.

## How do you time appetizers on Thanksgiving Day?

A simple timeline keeps starters from colliding with the turkey.

### The day before

- Make dips, spiced nuts, the cheese ball and any pickles.
- Cook and peel eggs for deviled eggs.
- Wash and cut vegetables, then store them in containers with a damp paper towel.

### Thanksgiving morning

- Fill deviled eggs and keep them chilled.
- Set out platters, bowls and serving spoons so assembly is fast.

### 30 minutes before guests arrive

- Arrange the cheese board and crudités.
- Start any warm appetizer in the slow cooker or small oven.

### While the turkey rests

This is the sweet spot. The turkey needs 20 to 30 minutes to rest before carving, and that is when people get hungry. Bring out a fresh plate of something then.

### Keep it safe

Cold dips, eggs and cheese should not sit out for more than 2 hours. Put out small batches and swap them for fresh ones from the fridge. Keep warm dips at 140°F (60°C) or above in a slow cooker on the warm setting.

## How do appetizers fit the rest of the Thanksgiving menu?

Think of the whole meal together. If dinner is heavy on creamy sides, keep starters crisp and tangy. If you are serving a lot of sweet dishes, a sharp cheese and pickles balance things out.
