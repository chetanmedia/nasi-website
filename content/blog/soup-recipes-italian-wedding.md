Italian wedding soup is tiny meatballs and leafy greens simmered in a light chicken broth with a small pasta like acini di pepe or orzo. To make it, roll small meatballs, soften onion, carrot and celery in a pot, add broth, then simmer the meatballs until cooked through and stir in greens and pasta at the end. It takes about 45 minutes and tastes like it took all afternoon.

Below you'll find one simple base method, a quick-reference table, and 18 variations grouped by time and mood, so you can make the version that fits your night.

## What is Italian wedding soup?

It's an Italian-American soup built on a simple idea: meat plus greens in broth. The name comes from "minestra maritata", or "married soup", because the meat and greens are said to go well together. It has nothing to do with weddings, though it's lovely enough to serve at one.

### What goes in a classic version?

- **Meatballs:** small, about 1 inch (2.5 cm), usually beef, pork or a mix, sometimes chicken or turkey, with breadcrumbs, egg, Parmesan and garlic.
- **Broth:** chicken broth, ideally a good one, since it's most of the soup.
- **Greens:** escarole is traditional, but spinach and kale are common.
- **Pasta:** acini di pepe, orzo, ditalini or small stars.
- **Aromatics:** onion, carrot, celery and garlic.
- **Finish:** grated Parmesan and black pepper.

## How do you make Italian wedding soup?

Here's the general method most versions follow. Adjust amounts to your taste and your pot.

### Step by step

1. **Mix and roll the meatballs.** Combine ground meat with breadcrumbs, a beaten egg, grated Parmesan, garlic, parsley, salt and pepper. Roll into small balls, about the size of a large marble.
2. **Brown or bake them (optional).** Browning in a skillet or baking on a sheet pan adds flavor. You can also drop them raw into simmering broth, which is easier and gives a softer meatball.
3. **Start the soup base.** Soften diced onion, carrot and celery in olive oil until tender, then add garlic for a minute.
4. **Add broth and simmer.** Pour in the broth and bring it to a gentle simmer.
5. **Cook the meatballs in the broth.** Add them and simmer until cooked through. Ground beef and pork should reach 160°F (71°C), and chicken or turkey 165°F (74°C).
6. **Add pasta and greens.** Cook the pasta in the broth (or separately) and stir in the greens until just wilted.
7. **Finish and serve.** Season, then serve with Parmesan on top.

### Quick-reference table

| Part | Typical amount for 6 servings | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Ground meat | 1 lb (450 g) | Roll first, 15 min |
| Chicken broth | 10 to 12 cups (2.4 to 2.8 L) | Simmer 10 min before meatballs |
| Small pasta | 3/4 cup (about 130 g) | Last 8 to 10 min |
| Greens | 4 to 6 cups loosely packed (about 120 to 180 g) | Last 2 to 3 min |
| Meatball doneness | 160°F (71°C) beef or pork, 165°F (74°C) poultry | About 8 to 12 min simmering |

## What are easy Italian wedding soup variations?

Once you know the base, it's easy to bend it toward what you have and how much time you've got.

### Weeknight shortcuts (30 minutes or less)

1. **Store-bought mini meatballs.** Use fully cooked frozen meatballs and simmer them in broth until hot all the way through.
2. **Sausage "meatballs".** Squeeze small pieces of Italian sausage straight from the casing into the broth. No mixing needed.
3. **Rotisserie chicken version.** Skip the meatballs, add shredded rotisserie chicken and a handful of Parmesan.
4. **Bagged spinach and orzo.** The quickest greens and pasta combo there is.
5. **Tortellini wedding soup.** Swap the small pasta for cheese tortellini for a heartier bowl.

### Lighter and leaner

6. **Chicken meatballs.** Ground chicken with Parmesan and herbs keeps it light.
7. **Turkey meatballs.** A bit leaner than beef and just as cozy.
8. **Extra-veg version.** Add zucchini, extra carrot and white beans, and use less pasta.
9. **Kale and lemon.** Sturdy kale and a squeeze of lemon at the end brighten the whole pot.

### Slow and cozy

10. **Slow cooker wedding soup.** Simmer broth, vegetables and browned meatballs on low, then add pasta and greens in the last 20 to 30 minutes.
11. **Homemade broth base.** Make your own chicken stock the day before for a deeper flavor.
12. **Parmesan rind broth.** Drop a Parmesan rind into the simmering broth, then remove it before serving.
13. **Escarole the traditional way.** Slightly bitter escarole gives the classic taste.

### Different moods

14. **Vegetarian wedding soup.** Use vegetable broth and small lentil, chickpea or mushroom balls.
15. **Gluten-free version.** Use gluten-free breadcrumbs and pasta, or skip the pasta and add rice.
16. **Spicy version.** Hot Italian sausage and a pinch of red pepper flakes.
17. **Pesto swirl.** Finish each bowl with a spoonful of pesto.
18. **Egg ribbons.** Drizzle beaten egg and Parmesan into the simmering broth for soft ribbons, like stracciatella.

## How do I store and reheat Italian wedding soup?

Cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours. They keep for about 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

### Does the pasta get mushy?

Yes, if it sits in the broth. The pasta keeps drinking up liquid, so day-two soup can turn thick. Two fixes: cook the pasta separately and add it to each bowl, or add a splash more broth when you reheat.

### Can I freeze it?

Freeze the soup without the pasta for up to about 3 months. Thaw in the fridge, reheat, and cook fresh pasta to add at the end.

## What should I serve with Italian wedding soup?

- Crusty bread or garlic bread
- A simple green salad with lemon dressing
- Grilled cheese for kids
- A plate of olives, cheese and cured meats for a bigger spread
