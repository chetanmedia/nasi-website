The best Christmas dinner ideas for large groups are dishes that scale up easily and can be made ahead: a baked ham or a big roast, oven-baked sides in 9x13 pans, a slow cooker or two, and desserts you finish the day before. Pick one or two mains, four or five sides, and set it up as a buffet. That way you spend Christmas with your people, not stuck at the stove.

Below are 24 ideas grouped by course, plus a simple game plan for feeding 12 to 25 people.

## What main dishes work best for a big Christmas crowd?

Choose mains that feed a lot of people with little hands-on time. One showpiece plus one easy backup covers most groups.

### Classic roasts

1. **Spiral-cut glazed ham.** Already cooked, so you're just warming it and brushing on a glaze. Feeds a crowd with almost no effort.
2. **Roast turkey or turkey breasts.** Two turkey breasts cook faster and more evenly than one giant bird. Cook to 165°F in the thickest part.
3. **Beef tenderloin.** Pricier, but it roasts in under an hour and slices beautifully for a buffet.
4. **Pork loin roast.** Affordable, quick to cook and easy to carve into neat slices.

### Hands-off mains

5. **Slow cooker pulled pork.** Serve with soft rolls and let people build their own sandwiches.
6. **Two or three roast chickens.** Easy to cook side by side, and most people love them.
7. **Baked pasta, like lasagna or baked ziti.** Make it a day ahead and bake before dinner. A great option for vegetarians, too.
8. **Big-batch stew or chili.** Unconventional for Christmas, but warm, filling and easy to hold on low heat.

## What are the best Christmas sides for a large group?

Sides are where a crowd dinner either flows or falls apart. Lean on dishes that bake in one pan and hold well. For more inspiration, see our full list of [Christmas side dishes](../christmas-dinner-ideas-sides/).

### Oven-baked sides

9. **Make-ahead mashed potatoes.** Mash them the day before, then reheat in the oven or a slow cooker with extra butter.
10. **Scalloped or au gratin potatoes.** Bakes in a 9x13 pan and stays hot for a long time.
11. **Sheet-pan roasted vegetables.** Carrots, Brussels sprouts and squash all roast at the same temperature.
12. **Green bean casserole.** Assemble ahead and bake when the oven frees up.
13. **Baked mac and cheese.** Kids and adults both go for it.
14. **Stuffing or dressing baked in a pan.** Easier to portion than stuffing cooked inside the bird.

### Make-ahead and no-cook sides

15. **Big winter salad.** Greens, pomegranate seeds, nuts and a simple vinaigrette. Dress it right before serving.
16. **Dinner rolls.** Bake from frozen or buy from a good bakery. Nobody will mind.
17. **Cranberry sauce.** Keeps for days in the fridge, so make it early.
18. **Antipasto or cheese board.** Doubles as an appetizer while people arrive.

## What desserts are easy to make for a crowd?

Desserts for a big group should be sliceable, portable and ready before the day starts.

19. **Sheet cake.** A 9x13 or half-sheet cake serves far more people than a layer cake.
20. **Christmas cookie platter.** Bake a few kinds over the week before. Our [Italian Christmas cookies](../christmas-cookies-italian/) are great for this since many keep well.
21. **Trifle in a big glass bowl.** Assembled ahead and looks festive with almost no skill.
22. **Pies.** Two or three pies, store-bought or homemade, cover a lot of people.
23. **Bread pudding.** Bakes in one pan and can be warmed up.
24. **Brownie or blondie bars.** Easy to cut into small squares so people can try a bit of everything.

## How much food do you need for a large Christmas dinner?

Planning amounts is the part that trips most hosts up. These are rough guides that work for a typical adult crowd. Make a little extra if you want leftovers.

| Food | Per person (approx.) | For 20 people |
|---|---|---|
| Bone-in turkey | 1 lb | About 20 lb |
| Boneless ham or beef | 1/2 lb | About 10 lb |
| Mashed potatoes | 1/2 lb raw potatoes | About 10 lb |
| Vegetable side | 1/2 cup cooked | About 10 cups |
| Dinner rolls | 1 to 2 rolls | 30 to 40 rolls |
| Gravy | 1/4 cup | About 5 cups |
| Dessert | 1 to 2 small servings | 30+ servings across dishes |

If you offer two mains, you can cut each one down a bit. People take smaller portions of each when there's more choice.

## How do you plan a large Christmas dinner without stress?

A good plan is mostly about timing. Here's a simple countdown.

### One to two weeks before

- Settle the headcount and the menu.
- Ask guests to bring specific dishes, like a salad, rolls or a dessert. Be specific so you don't end up with four pies and no vegetables.
- Order the ham, turkey or roast. Big cuts sell out close to Christmas.

### Two to three days before

- Thaw a frozen turkey in the fridge. Allow about 1 day for every 4 to 5 pounds.
- Shop for everything else.
- Bake cookies and make cranberry sauce.

### The day before

- Make mashed potatoes, casseroles and the trifle.
- Set out serving dishes with a sticky note on each saying what goes in it.
- Clear fridge space.

### Christmas Day

- Put the main in the oven first and work backward from serving time.
- Reheat sides while the meat rests.
- Set up the buffet and let people serve themselves.

## How do you keep food safe at a big holiday buffet?

When lots of food sits out, food safety matters more than usual.

- Keep hot food at 140°F or above. Slow cookers on warm and covered dishes in a low oven help.
- Keep cold food at 40°F or below. Nest bowls of salad or dip in a tray of ice.
- Don't leave perishable food out for more than 2 hours. Put out smaller platters and refill them from the kitchen.
- Cool leftovers quickly. Divide them into shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours.

## Quick recap

- Pick one or two scalable mains, like ham, turkey breasts or a big pasta bake.
- Choose sides that bake in one pan or hold in a slow cooker.
- Make desserts and as many sides as you can the day before.
- Serve buffet style and keep hot food hot and cold food cold.
- Ask guests to bring specific dishes so the menu stays balanced.
