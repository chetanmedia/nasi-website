The best apps like ReciMe are Nasi for recipes that start as TikTok, Instagram or Facebook videos, Paprika and Copy Me That for recipes clipped from websites, and Samsung Food or Mealime if you mostly want meal planning. Several of them have free plans, so you can try one with your own saved recipes before you pay. Below we compare six ReciMe alternatives side by side and explain who each one suits.

## Why do people look for apps like ReciMe?

ReciMe is a popular recipe organizer that saves recipes from social platforms and websites and adds meal planning and grocery lists. Plenty of cooks are happy with it. People usually go looking for an alternative for one of a few reasons.

### Common reasons to switch

- **Price.** ReciMe has a free tier and a paid subscription. Limits and prices have changed over time, so some people want a cheaper or more generous free option.
- **Video recipes.** Some cooks want an app that pays close attention to what the creator actually says and does in the video, not only the caption.
- **Platform.** Some want desktop or web access. Others want something simpler on their phone.
- **Focus.** Some people only need a recipe archive, and others only need meal planning.

### A note on prices

We list prices only where we could confirm them on the app's own site or store listing. For ReciMe and others where pricing varies by region or has changed recently, open the paywall in the app to see what applies to you today.

## What does each ReciMe alternative do best?

### Nasi: the closest match for video recipes

Nasi is built for the recipes you find while scrolling. Share a TikTok, Instagram Reel or Facebook video to Nasi, or paste the link. Nasi watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption, then writes the ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator.

You can also scan a photo, screenshot, cookbook page or handwritten card, upload a PDF (one recipe per PDF), paste text, or type a recipe by hand. Saved recipes go into searchable cookbooks, and you can scale servings, add ingredients to your shopping list, plan the week and cook hands-free.

**Pick it if:** most of your saved recipes are reels.
**Skip it if:** you need Android, or you mostly save from recipe websites. Nasi does not open website, YouTube or Pinterest links.

### Paprika: the classic for website recipes

Paprika has been a favorite for years. Its built-in browser grabs recipes from websites, and recipes live on your device, so you can cook offline. It is a one-time purchase per platform rather than a subscription.

**Pick it if:** you clip from blogs and want to pay once.
**Skip it if:** your recipes come mostly from social video.

### Copy Me That: the budget web clipper

Copy Me That clips recipes from websites with a browser extension or its app. The free tier is genuinely useful, and Premium is $12.99 a year.

**Pick it if:** you want a low-cost home for website recipes.
**Skip it if:** you need video import.

### Recipe Keeper: a digital recipe box for the long haul

Recipe Keeper feels like the recipe box your family kept for decades, just on your phone. You can import from websites, type recipes in and sort them by category. The free version has a recipe cap, and a one-time Pro upgrade removes it. We compare the two head to head in [ReciMe vs Recipe Keeper](../recime-vs-recipe-keeper/).

**Pick it if:** you are building a family collection that will last.
**Skip it if:** you want social video import.

### Samsung Food: free planning and discovery

Samsung Food is free to use for saving website recipes, planning meals and making shopping lists, with a paid tier for extras.

**Pick it if:** meal planning matters more to you than a private archive.
**Skip it if:** you want a quiet place for just your own recipes.

### Mealime: plans made for you

Mealime builds a meal plan and grocery list from its own recipe library. It is more planner than organizer.

**Pick it if:** you want weeknight decisions made for you.
**Skip it if:** you want to store recipes you found elsewhere.

## Are there free apps like ReciMe?

Yes, though "free" means different things in each app.

### Free with a weekly limit

Nasi is free with 3 imports every week, and you keep everything you have saved. That suits people who add a few new recipes a week.

### Free with a total cap

Recipe Keeper's free version limits how many recipes you can store. Fine at first, tighter as your collection grows.

### Free with paid extras

Copy Me That, Samsung Food and Mealime are free to use, with paid tiers for extra features.

## How do you choose the right ReciMe alternative?

Look at the last ten recipes you saved and ask where they came from.

### Mostly TikTok, Instagram or Facebook

Choose an app that reads video. Captions often skip amounts, and screenshots miss steps. Nasi is the most focused option here. Our guide on [how to save recipes from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook](../how-to-save-recipes-from-tiktok-instagram-facebook/) shows the full flow.

### Mostly recipe websites

Paprika or Copy Me That. Both strip the long story above the recipe card and keep just what you need.

### A mix of everything

Either keep ReciMe, or pair a video app like Nasi with a web clipper. Two focused apps can be easier than one that does everything halfway.

### Mostly planning

Samsung Food or Mealime. You can still keep your saved favorites in a separate organizer.

## How do you switch apps without losing recipes?

### Start with your favorites

Do not try to move everything. Re-save your ten most-cooked recipes first. Most people cook from a small rotation anyway.

### Use the original source

If a recipe came from a reel, share the original video into your new app. You get a fresh, complete copy with a link back to the creator.

### Screenshot the rest

For recipes you cannot find again, take screenshots in your old app and scan them into the new one. Nasi, for example, can turn a screenshot into a clean recipe.

### Keep the old app for a few weeks

Do not delete anything until you have cooked from the new app a few times and you are sure nothing is missing.
