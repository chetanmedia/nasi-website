The best Thanksgiving recipes in a crockpot are the sides that only need gentle, steady heat: mashed potatoes, stuffing, mac and cheese, sweet potatoes, creamed corn, green beans, cranberry sauce and gravy. Move two or three of these into slow cookers and your oven is free for the turkey and the pies. Below are 24 ideas, sorted by when you can start them, plus a simple timing plan.

## Why use a crockpot for Thanksgiving?

Thanksgiving has one bottleneck, and it is the oven. The turkey takes it over for hours, and then every casserole wants in at the same time. A slow cooker is a second, third or fourth burner that sits on the counter and asks for nothing.

It helps in three ways:

- **It frees the oven.** Anything that can cook low and slow on the counter is one less dish fighting for a rack.
- **It keeps food warm.** Once a side is done, the warm setting holds it while you carve, so nothing goes cold on the table.
- **It frees your hands.** You can start a dish in the morning and leave it alone while you deal with the bird.

If you are still building your list of dishes, start with our [Thanksgiving menu plan](../thanksgiving-recipes-menu/), then come back and decide which ones move to the slow cooker.

## Which Thanksgiving dishes work best in a slow cooker?

Good crockpot dishes are soft, saucy or creamy. They cook in liquid, or they are fine being stirred and held. Dishes that need browning or crunch do better in the oven.

### Great in a crockpot

- Mashed potatoes, regular or sweet
- Stuffing and dressing
- Mac and cheese
- Creamed corn and corn pudding
- Green beans with onions or bacon
- Cranberry sauce
- Gravy and warm drinks like cider

### Better in the oven

- A whole turkey (too big to heat safely in a slow cooker)
- Roasted vegetables you want caramelized
- Anything with a crisp topping or crust
- Rolls and pies

## What can I make in a crockpot the day before?

These ideas cook ahead, then reheat in the slow cooker on low on the day. Cool them quickly, refrigerate in shallow containers, and reheat until steaming hot, 165°F (74°C).

### Make-ahead sides and sauces

1. **Cranberry sauce.** Fresh cranberries, sugar and orange simmered until the berries burst. It sets as it cools and keeps for days.
2. **Apple butter.** Apples cooked down very slowly with warm spices. Good on rolls, or as a gift for the host.
3. **Turkey stock.** Wings or necks with onion, carrot and celery, cooked low all day. It becomes the base for your gravy and stuffing.
4. **Make-ahead gravy.** Built from that stock and a butter and flour roux, then held on warm. Whisk in turkey drippings at the end if you have them.
5. **Caramelized onions.** Sliced onions with butter, cooked low with the lid cracked. Stir them into green beans, stuffing or mashed potatoes.
6. **Chili or soup for the night before.** A pot that feeds the early arrivals so you are not cooking two big meals in a row.

## What can I start in the crockpot on Thanksgiving morning?

These are the main event. Start them in the morning and they will be ready around the time the turkey comes out to rest.

### Potatoes and starches

7. **Crockpot mashed potatoes.** Cubed potatoes cooked in broth until tender, then mashed right in the pot with butter and warm milk. No big pot of boiling water on the stove.
8. **Sweet potato casserole.** Mashed sweet potatoes with butter and spice. Add a pecan or marshmallow topping at the end, or toast it briefly under the broiler in a separate dish.
9. **Slow cooker stuffing.** Dried bread cubes, sautéed onion and celery, herbs and stock. Cook it with a paper towel under the lid to catch drips so the top does not go soggy.
10. **Crockpot mac and cheese.** A creamy, no-boil version. Stir once or twice so the edges do not overcook.
11. **Cornbread dressing.** Crumbled cornbread with sausage or just vegetables. See our [dressing and stuffing guide](../dressing-for-thanksgiving-recipes/) for flavor ideas.
12. **Wild rice pilaf.** Wild rice, mushrooms and stock. A good gluten-free side if you use gluten-free stock.

### Vegetables

13. **Green beans with bacon and onions.** Fresh or frozen beans cooked low with smoky bits. For more takes, see our [Thanksgiving green bean recipes](../thanksgiving-recipes-green-beans/).
14. **Creamed corn.** Corn, cream cheese and butter. One of the easiest crockpot sides there is.
15. **Honey-glazed carrots.** Carrots with butter, honey and a little thyme.
16. **Corn pudding.** A soft, spoonable side that holds well on warm.
17. **Braised red cabbage.** Cabbage, apple and a splash of vinegar. It cuts through all the rich food on the plate.

### Mains

18. **Crockpot turkey breast.** A bone-in or boneless breast cooked on low until the thickest part reads 165°F (74°C). Good for a small group or as extra white meat. Crisp the skin under the broiler if you like.
19. **Brown sugar ham.** A fully cooked spiral ham warmed through with a simple glaze. Heat it to 140°F (60°C).
20. **Mushroom and lentil stew.** A hearty vegetarian main that also works as a side.

## What warm drinks and desserts can I make in a crockpot?

Once the savory dishes are done, a slow cooker can switch to drinks or dessert. This is a nice job for a second, smaller crockpot.

### Drinks

21. **Mulled apple cider.** Cider with cinnamon sticks, cloves and orange slices, held on warm all afternoon so guests can serve themselves.
22. **Hot chocolate bar.** Rich hot cocoa on warm with toppings set out next to it. Kids love it.

### Desserts

23. **Pumpkin pie pudding.** All the flavor of pumpkin pie without the crust. Serve with whipped cream.
24. **Apple crisp.** Spiced apples in the crock, topped with an oat crumble. The topping will be soft rather than crunchy, which many people like.

## How do I plan a crockpot Thanksgiving timeline?

Write the plan down. It sounds fussy, but it is the thing that stops a dish from being forgotten in a corner.

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 2 days before | Make stock and cranberry sauce. Cool and refrigerate. |
| Day before | Make gravy and any make-ahead sides. Prep vegetables and bread cubes. |
| Morning, 6 to 7 hours before dinner | Start the turkey breast or ham if using. |
| 4 hours before dinner | Start stuffing, mashed potatoes or mac and cheese. |
| 2 to 3 hours before dinner | Start quick vegetable sides like creamed corn or carrots. |
| 1 hour before dinner | Reheat gravy. Switch finished dishes to warm. |
| Dinner | Serve straight from the crocks. Start cider for dessert. |

Cook times vary by slow cooker. Newer models often run hotter than older ones, so check early the first time you try a recipe. For a broader take on planning food across a whole week, our [weekly meal planning guide](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/) uses the same work-backwards idea.

## How do I keep slow cooker food safe on Thanksgiving?

A slow cooker is safe when you use it the way it was designed. A few rules matter most.

### Start with thawed food

Never put frozen meat or a frozen turkey breast into a slow cooker. It can sit too long in the temperature danger zone before it heats through. Thaw it in the fridge first.

### Fill it right

Most slow cookers work best half to two-thirds full. Too little and food can scorch. Too much and it may not heat evenly.

### Keep the lid on

Every time you lift the lid, heat escapes and the cook time stretches. Stir only when the recipe asks.

### Check temperatures

Use a meat thermometer. Poultry is done at 165°F (74°C). Reheated leftovers and make-ahead dishes should also reach 165°F (74°C). Hold cooked food at 140°F (60°C) or above on the warm setting.

### Put leftovers away on time

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of serving. Do not cool food in the slow cooker insert, because the thick crock holds heat for a long time. Move food to shallow containers instead.

## How many crockpots do I need for Thanksgiving?

For most families, two or three is plenty. One for a starch like mashed potatoes, one for stuffing, and a small one for gravy or cranberry sauce. If you only have one, use it for mashed potatoes, which take up the most stovetop space otherwise.

Ask guests to bring their own slow cooker with their dish. It solves the "where does this go" problem and keeps the food warm until dinner. Put a strip of tape on each lid with the owner's name so everything goes home with the right person.
