The best Italian Christmas cookies for most home bakers are biscotti, ricotta cookies, pizzelle and amaretti. They cover crunchy, soft, crisp and chewy, and most keep well enough to bake ahead. Below are 20 Italian cookie ideas grouped by effort, with tips for building a tray that looks generous without taking over your week.

These are general descriptions to help you choose. Recipes vary a lot by region and family, so treat this as a shortlist, then use a recipe you trust. ## What makes a cookie an Italian Christmas cookie?

Most Italian holiday cookies lean on a few familiar flavors: almond, anise, citrus zest, honey, dried figs and warm spices. Many are less sweet than American cookies and are made to be dunked in coffee or a glass of wine.

Texture varies more than you might expect. Some are dry and crunchy, made to keep for weeks. Others are soft and cakey with a simple glaze. A good tray mixes the two.

### A note on regional names

The same cookie can go by different names in different parts of Italy and in Italian American bakeries. Fig-filled cookies, for example, are known as cuccidati in Sicily, and you will see many spellings. Do not worry about getting the name exactly right. Focus on flavor and texture.

## What are the easiest Italian Christmas cookies?

These are simple doughs with forgiving methods. Good for a first batch or for baking with kids.

### Drop and rolled cookies

1. **Ricotta cookies.** Soft, cakey drops topped with a simple glaze and sprinkles.
2. **Anise cookies.** Lightly sweet with a licorice note, often glazed and finished with nonpareils.
3. **Italian wedding cookies.** Buttery nut balls rolled in powdered sugar.
4. **Lemon drop cookies.** Soft and tender, with a sharp lemon icing.
5. **Chocolate spice cookies.** Rich, cocoa-based balls with warm spices and a glaze, sometimes called tutu or toto.

### Shape once, slice later

6. **Almond biscotti.** One log, baked, sliced and baked again until crisp.
7. **Chocolate hazelnut biscotti.** The same method with cocoa and toasted hazelnuts.
8. **Cranberry pistachio biscotti.** Red and green, so they look festive without any decorating.

## Which Italian cookies take a little more effort?

These need a special tool, a filling or a bit more patience, but they are worth it.

### Pressed and piped cookies

9. **Pizzelle.** Thin, crisp waffle cookies made in a pizzelle iron, often flavored with anise or vanilla.
10. **Italian butter cookies.** Piped rosettes and S shapes, sometimes dipped in chocolate.
11. **Amaretti.** Chewy or crisp almond cookies made with egg whites, ground almonds and sugar.
12. **Ricciarelli.** Soft almond cookies from Siena with a crackled, powdered top.

### Filled and layered cookies

13. **Cuccidati.** Sicilian cookies with a spiced fig, raisin and nut filling wrapped in pastry.
14. **Rainbow cookies.** Three thin almond sponge layers with jam, topped with chocolate. A bakery classic in the US.
15. **Sesame cookies.** Simple dough logs rolled in sesame seeds, nutty and not too sweet.
16. **Jam thumbprints with almond.** A buttery dough with a dot of apricot or raspberry jam.

## What other Italian holiday sweets belong on the tray?

Not every tray item has to be a classic cookie. These add variety and are popular at Italian Christmas tables.

17. **Struffoli.** Tiny fried dough balls coated in warm honey and sprinkles, piled into a ring or mound.
18. **Panforte squares.** A dense, chewy slab of nuts, honey and spice. Cut it into small pieces.
19. **Torrone.** Nougat with almonds or hazelnuts, often bought rather than made, and easy to cut into bites.
20. **Chocolate salami.** A no-bake roll of chocolate and crushed biscuits, sliced into rounds.

## How far ahead can you bake Italian Christmas cookies?

Plan your baking around how well each cookie keeps.

| Cookie | Keeps at room temp (airtight) | Freezes well? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biscotti | 2 to 3 weeks | Yes |
| Amaretti | About 1 week | Yes |
| Ricciarelli | About 1 week | Yes |
| Pizzelle | About 1 week, kept dry | Yes, flat with parchment |
| Ricotta cookies | 3 to 4 days | Yes, before glazing |
| Rainbow cookies | About 1 week in the fridge | Yes |
| Cuccidati | 1 to 2 weeks | Yes |

A simple plan is to bake biscotti and cuccidati first, two to three weeks out. Freeze anything soft, like ricotta cookies, and glaze them after they thaw. Bake pizzelle and amaretti in the last week.

Keep crisp and soft cookies in separate tins. If you store them together, the crisp ones soften.

## How do you build an Italian Christmas cookie tray?

Aim for five to seven kinds. That is enough variety to feel generous without turning December into a baking marathon.

### Mix textures and colors

- One **crunchy** cookie, like biscotti or pizzelle.
- One **soft** cookie, like ricotta or lemon drops.
- One **chewy almond** cookie, like amaretti or ricciarelli.
- One **filled or layered** cookie, like rainbow cookies or cuccidati.
- One **powdered or glazed** cookie for contrast, like wedding cookies.

### Arrange it

Start with the largest cookies around the edge. Fill in with smaller pieces, then tuck torrone or chocolate salami into the gaps. A few sprigs of rosemary or some citrus peel make it look finished.
