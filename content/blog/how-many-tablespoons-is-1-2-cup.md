There are **8 tablespoons in 1/2 cup**. That is the same as 24 teaspoons, 4 fluid ounces, or about 120 mL. If your half-cup measure is in the dishwasher, fill a tablespoon eight times, leveling each one, and you are there.

Below is a quick chart, the simple math behind it, and a few tricks for butter, halving and doubling.

## What does 1/2 cup equal in tablespoons, teaspoons and ounces?

These are US measures, with metric in brackets. Level every spoon with the back of a knife.

| Cups | Tablespoons (tbsp) | Teaspoons (tsp) | Fluid ounces | Milliliters (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/8 cup | 2 tbsp | 6 tsp | 1 fl oz | 30 mL |
| 1/4 cup | 4 tbsp | 12 tsp | 2 fl oz | 60 mL |
| 1/3 cup | 5 tbsp + 1 tsp | 16 tsp | 2 2/3 fl oz | 80 mL |
| 1/2 cup | 8 tbsp | 24 tsp | 4 fl oz | 120 mL |
| 2/3 cup | 10 tbsp + 2 tsp | 32 tsp | 5 1/3 fl oz | 160 mL |
| 3/4 cup | 12 tbsp | 36 tsp | 6 fl oz | 180 mL |
| 1 cup | 16 tbsp | 48 tsp | 8 fl oz | 240 mL |

For the smaller versions of this chart, see [how many tablespoons in 1/4 cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-1-4-c/) and [how many tablespoons in 1/3 cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-1-3-cup/). For bigger amounts, [how many tablespoons in a cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-a-cup/) has the full picture.

## Why is 1/2 cup exactly 8 tablespoons?

A US cup holds 16 tablespoons. Half of 16 is 8. That is the whole trick.

### The spoon ladder

It helps to remember the chain:

- 3 teaspoons = 1 tablespoon
- 2 tablespoons = 1 fluid ounce (1/8 cup)
- 4 tablespoons = 1/4 cup
- 8 tablespoons = 1/2 cup
- 16 tablespoons = 1 cup

Once that sticks, you rarely need to look anything up. Our guide to [how many teaspoons are in a tablespoon](../teaspoon-is-how-many-tablespoons/) goes one step smaller.

### What about metric cups?

A US cup is about 240 mL, so half is about 120 mL. Some countries use a 250 mL metric cup, which makes half a cup 125 mL. The difference is about 1 teaspoon, which will not matter in soups or sauces. In baking, use the cup system the recipe was written in, or weigh.

## How do you measure 1/2 cup of butter?

In the US, 1/2 cup of butter is one standard stick. That is 8 tablespoons, or about 4 ounces (113 grams).

### Using the wrapper

Most butter wrappers have tablespoon lines printed on them. Cut along the lines for any amount you need, from 1 tablespoon to the full stick.

### Melted or softened?

If a recipe says "1/2 cup butter, melted", measure first, then melt. A stick melts down to roughly 1/2 cup of liquid, so you are fine either way. Softened butter can be pressed into a dry measuring cup, but the stick is faster and cleaner.

## Does 1/2 cup weigh the same for every ingredient?

No. Tablespoons and cups measure volume, not weight. Half a cup of flour and half a cup of sugar take up the same space but weigh very different amounts.

### Rough weights for 1/2 cup

| Ingredient | 1/2 cup weighs about |
|---|---|
| Water or milk | 4 oz (120 g) |
| Butter | 4 oz (113 g) |
| Granulated sugar | 3.5 oz (100 g) |
| Brown sugar, packed | 3.7 oz (105 g) |
| All-purpose flour, spooned and leveled | 2.1 oz (60 g) |
| Rolled oats | 1.6 oz (45 g) |

These are common averages. Flour in particular changes with how you scoop it. For exact grams by ingredient, use our [kitchen converter](../../tools/kitchen-converter/).

### Dry vs liquid measuring cups

For flour and sugar, use a dry measuring cup and level it off. For milk, water and oil, use a clear liquid measuring cup on a flat counter and read it at eye level. Both hold the same volume, but each is easier to fill accurately with the right kind of ingredient.

## How do you halve or double 1/2 cup?

This is where tablespoons save the day.

### Halving

Half of 1/2 cup is 1/4 cup, or 4 tablespoons. Half again is 2 tablespoons. If a recipe uses 1/2 cup of something and you are making half a batch, grab the 1/4 cup and move on.

### Doubling and tripling

- 2 x 1/2 cup = 1 cup (16 tbsp)
- 3 x 1/2 cup = 1 1/2 cups (24 tbsp)
- 4 x 1/2 cup = 2 cups (32 tbsp)

### A quick tip for sticky ingredients

Honey, molasses and peanut butter cling to cups. Wipe the cup or spoon with a little oil first and they slide right out, so your 8 tablespoons really are 8 tablespoons.
