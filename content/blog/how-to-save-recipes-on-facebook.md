To save a recipe on Facebook, tap the three dots on the post or video and choose **Save**. It goes to your Saved list, which you can find in the Facebook menu and sort into collections. That keeps a bookmark, though, not the recipe itself, so if you want to cook from it later, it pays to keep a written copy too.

Facebook is full of good food. Recipe videos in your feed, Reels from creators you follow, and those huge recipe groups where someone's grandma posts her pie crust. Here's how to save all of it, and how to make sure it's still there when you actually want to cook.

## How do you save a recipe video on Facebook?

Saving a video takes about two seconds.

### Save it from the post

1. Find the recipe video or Reel in your feed.
2. Tap the three dots in the top corner of the post. On Reels, the menu sits on the side of the screen.
3. Choose **Save video** (or **Save post**, depending on what it is).

That's it. Facebook adds it to your Saved list.

### Find your saved recipes later

Open the Facebook menu (your profile picture or the three lines, depending on your device) and tap **Saved**. You'll see everything you've saved, newest first, with separate filters for videos, posts and links.

If you can't find **Saved** right away, use the search box in the menu. Facebook moves things around now and then, but the feature itself has been around for years.

## How do you save recipes from Facebook groups?

Recipe groups are some of the best places on Facebook to find home cooking. Members post family recipes, weeknight dinners and holiday baking, often as a photo plus a long block of text.

You save these the same way:

1. Open the group post.
2. Tap the three dots.
3. Choose **Save post**.

### A word of warning about groups

Group posts are fragile. If you leave the group, the group goes private, an admin removes the post, or the person who shared it deletes it, your saved copy can stop working. You'll tap it and see a message that the content isn't available.

So if a group recipe is one you know you'll make again, copy it out the same day you find it. Screenshot the text, or select and copy it into your notes. It takes a minute and saves a lot of frustration in December.

## How do you organize saved recipes into collections?

A single Saved list fills up fast, and recipes end up mixed in with memes, articles and that couch you almost bought. Collections fix this.

### Make a recipe collection

1. When you save something, Facebook often offers to add it to a collection. Tap **Add to collection** or **New collection**.
2. Give it a clear name, like "Weeknight dinners" or "Baking."
3. Next time you save a recipe, pick that collection.

You can also open your Saved list, tap the three dots on any item, and move it into a collection after the fact.

### Collections that actually help

Keep it simple. A few broad collections work better than twenty tiny ones:

- **Weeknight dinners** for anything under 45 minutes
- **Baking** for cookies, breads and cakes
- **Holidays** for Thanksgiving and Christmas ideas
- **Try this weekend** for bigger projects

You can share a collection with someone else, too, which is handy if you plan meals with a partner or roommate.

## Why do saved Facebook recipes stop working?

This is the part most guides skip. A Facebook save is a pointer to someone else's post. You don't own it. A few things can break it:

- **The creator deletes it.** Creators clean up old content all the time.
- **Privacy changes.** If the poster limits who can see it, you may lose access.
- **Group changes.** Leaving a group, or a group going private or closing, can cut you off.
- **Account changes.** If the creator's page is removed or renamed, links can break.

There's also a practical problem. Even when the save works, you still have a video, not a recipe. You have to scrub back and forth to catch "about a cup of broth" while your onions are burning. Ingredient amounts are often spoken, not written, and the steps go by fast.

## How do you turn a Facebook recipe into a written recipe?

You have a few options, from free and manual to quick and automatic.

### Write it down by hand

Watch the video once all the way through. Then watch again and pause after each step. Write the ingredients first, with amounts, then the steps in order. Check the post text for anything you missed, since creators often add oven temperatures or swaps there.

It works, but it's slow. A one-minute video can easily take ten minutes to write up properly.

### Copy the post text

For group posts and text-heavy recipes, press and hold the text, select it, and copy it into your notes app or a document. Clean up the formatting while it's fresh. If the post is just a photo of a recipe card, take a screenshot instead.

### Use a recipe app

A recipe app gives your saved recipes a proper home, with search, folders and a shopping list. Some apps can read the video for you. Our guide on [saving recipes from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook](../how-to-save-recipes-from-tiktok-instagram-facebook/) walks through the options across all three platforms.

## What's the best way to keep Facebook recipes for good?

The best system is one where the recipe lives somewhere you control, in a format you can cook from. Here's a quick comparison:

| Method | Keeps the recipe if the post is deleted? | Easy to cook from? | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook Save | No | No, you scrub the video | Very low |
| Facebook collections | No | No | Low |
| Screenshots | Yes | Somewhat | Low |
| Writing it out by hand | Yes | Yes | High |
| Recipe app import | Yes | Yes | Low |

A good habit: save to Facebook when you're scrolling, then once a week, move the ones you'll actually make into your permanent recipe collection. Delete the rest. You'll end up with a short list of recipes you trust instead of hundreds of saves you'll never open.

If you also save from other apps, the same approach works for [Instagram Reels](../how-to-save-recipes-on-instagram/) and [TikTok](../how-to-save-recipes-on-tiktok/), so all your recipes end up in one place.

## How does Nasi save recipes from Facebook?

[Nasi](../../) is an iPhone app built for exactly this. When you find a recipe video on Facebook, tap **Share**, then pick Nasi, or copy the link and paste it into the app. Nasi watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption. Then it writes out the ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator so they get the credit.

For group posts that are mostly text or a photo of a handwritten card, take a screenshot and scan it in Nasi, or copy the text and paste it. Either way, you get a clean recipe you can search, scale and add to your shopping list.

When it's time to cook, hands-free cook mode keeps the screen awake, shows one step at a time, and runs timers. You can say "next" or "back" instead of touching your phone with messy hands.

Nasi is free with 3 imports every week. Nasi Pro, with unlimited imports, is $29.99 a year after a 3-day free trial.

## Quick recap

- Tap the three dots and choose **Save** on any Facebook recipe post or video.
- Find saves in the menu under **Saved**, and sort them into a few simple collections.
- Group posts can disappear, so copy or screenshot the ones you love right away.
- For recipes you'll make again, keep a written copy somewhere you control.
