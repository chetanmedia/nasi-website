Yes, ReciMe is free to download and has a free version, but it is limited. The free version caps how many recipes you can bring in with its smart import, and the paid plan, ReciMe Plus, lifts that cap. In fall 2026, third-party price trackers listed ReciMe Plus at $39.99 a year in the US, with a free trial first.

That is the short answer. The longer one depends on how many recipes you save each week, where they come from, and whether you want meal planning and grocery lists on top. Below we walk through what the free version does, what you pay for, and when a different app might fit you better.

## Is ReciMe really free?

ReciMe uses a freemium model. You can download it from the App Store or Google Play, make an account and start saving recipes without paying. Nothing forces you to start a trial.

### What the free version is good for

The free version works well if you save a handful of recipes and cook from them now and then. You can:

- Import a limited number of recipes from social posts and websites.
- Type recipes in by hand.
- Browse and cook from the recipes you have saved.

### Where the free version runs out

The limit you will hit first is imports. ReciMe's smart import is the feature most people download it for, the one that turns a reel or a web page into a tidy recipe. On the free version, that import is capped.

Reports of the exact cap differ. One price tracker described it as a weekly allowance, and an older review described a fixed total before you had to upgrade. Limits like this change as apps test their pricing, so the honest advice is to check the app itself. If you save more than a few recipes a week, you will probably reach the limit quickly.

## How much does ReciMe cost?

The paid plan is called ReciMe Plus. It is billed through Apple or Google as an in-app subscription.

### The current yearly price

RecurDash, a subscription price tracker, listed ReciMe Plus at **$39.99 a year** in the US when it checked prices in October 2026. App Pricing Lab showed the same $39.99 option among ReciMe's in-app purchases. ReciMe also has other in-app purchase options in its listing, which can include different terms or regional prices.

You may still see older numbers online. ReciMe's own blog has mentioned a much lower yearly price in the past, which no longer matches the current listings. Treat any price you read in a blog post, including this one, as a guide. The upgrade screen in the app shows the price that applies to you.

### Is there a ReciMe free trial?

Yes. ReciMe Plus has offered a free trial, reported as 7 days. Like most App Store trials, it turns into a paid subscription unless you cancel before it ends.

### How to check the price on your own phone

1. Open ReciMe and look for the upgrade or Plus button.
2. Read the plan options and the trial length on the paywall.
3. On iPhone, you can also open the App Store listing and scroll to In-App Purchases to see every price option.

## What do you get with ReciMe Plus?

The main reason to pay is to stop counting imports. Paid recipe apps usually also put extra planning tools and convenience features behind the subscription. ReciMe's feature list includes meal planning and grocery lists, and the split between free and paid features shifts over time, so check the comparison screen in the app before you start a trial.

### Who should pay for ReciMe Plus

ReciMe Plus makes sense if:

- You save recipes from both social media and recipe websites, and you want them in one app.
- You save more recipes each week than the free cap allows.
- You will actually use its planning and grocery features every week.

If you only save a recipe or two a week, the free version may be all you need.

## Is ReciMe worth paying for?

It can be. The question is not whether the app is good. It is whether you will use the paid features enough to justify a yearly charge.

### A simple test before you subscribe

Try this for a week on the free version:

1. Save every recipe you would normally screenshot or bookmark.
2. Count how many you tried to import.
3. Note whether you used meal planning or the grocery list at all.

If you blew past the import limit and used the planner, ReciMe Plus is likely worth it for you. If you saved three recipes and never opened the planner, stay on free or look at a cheaper option.

### When a free recipe app is enough

A free plan is enough when you save a few recipes a week and mostly need them in one searchable place. Our list of [free recipe organizer apps](../recipe-organizer-app-free/) covers options that do not need a subscription at all.

## How do you cancel ReciMe or its free trial?

Because ReciMe bills through the App Store or Google Play, you cancel there, not inside the app.

### On iPhone

1. Open the Settings app.
2. Tap your name at the top.
3. Tap Subscriptions.
4. Pick ReciMe, then tap Cancel Subscription.

### On Android

1. Open the Google Play Store.
2. Tap your profile picture, then Payments and subscriptions.
3. Tap Subscriptions, pick ReciMe, and tap Cancel.

Cancel at least a day before the trial ends. You usually keep access until the end of the period you have already paid for.

### Before you cancel, save your recipes

Look in ReciMe's settings or help pages for any export or share option before you leave. If you cannot bulk export, re-save your favorites one by one. You can share each recipe, screenshot it, or go back to the original video.

## What are the cheaper alternatives to ReciMe?

If price is the main reason you searched this, here are the honest options.

### If your recipes come from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook

This is where Nasi fits. You share a TikTok, Instagram Reel or Facebook video to Nasi, or paste the link. Nasi watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption. Then it writes out the ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator.

Nasi is free with 3 imports every week, and that allowance resets weekly. Nasi Pro is $29.99 a year, which works out to $2.50 a month, with a 3-day free trial. Pro adds unlimited imports, smart scanning, cook mode, and sync and backup. It is iPhone only.

Nasi does not open recipe website, blog, YouTube or Pinterest links. For a recipe from one of those, you can screenshot the page and scan it, or copy the text and paste it in. If most of your recipes come from websites, a web clipper will serve you better.

Our step-by-step guide on [saving recipes from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook](../how-to-save-recipes-from-tiktok-instagram-facebook/) shows the full share-sheet flow.

### If your recipes come from websites

Paprika is a one-time purchase on each platform, so there is no yearly bill. Copy Me That and Recipe Keeper have free versions that cover the basics. Check each app's store page for current prices before you buy.

### If you want meal planning more than saving

Look at what you actually use. Some people pay for a recipe saver and only use the meal planner. In that case, a dedicated planning app, or a recipe saver with a simple weekly plan, may be cheaper.
