ReciMe is a solid recipe saver for people who find most of their recipes on TikTok, Instagram and recipe websites. Its import is fast and usually accurate when the recipe is in the caption, and it bundles meal planning and grocery lists. The weak spots are videos where the recipe is only spoken, a free tier with a tight import cap, and a yearly price that sits above some rivals.

That is the short version of our ReciMe review. Below we walk through what it does well, where it falls short, what it costs, and who should pick something else.

## What is ReciMe?

ReciMe is a recipe manager for iPhone and Android. You send it a recipe from social media or the web, and it turns that post into a recipe card with ingredients and steps. On top of that it adds a cookbook to organize recipes, a meal planner and a grocery list.

### Where can ReciMe import recipes from?

ReciMe's App Store listing says it saves recipes from Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and websites. You can also type a recipe in by hand. That wide source list is one of its main selling points, and it is a big reason people compare it with other apps in our [best recipe app roundup](../recipe-app-best/).

### Who makes it?

ReciMe is made by a small app company and is sold as a freemium app. You can use it free, and a paid plan called ReciMe Plus lifts the limits. We cover the money side in more detail in [Is ReciMe free?](../is-recime-app-free/).

## How well does ReciMe import recipes?

Import is the feature you are really paying for, so it gets the most attention here.

### Caption-based imports are strong

When a creator writes the full recipe in the caption, ReciMe does well. Independent reviewers, including WhistleOut, found it built a full ingredient list and numbered steps in seconds and got most videos right over weeks of testing. For a typical Instagram post with a written recipe, you can expect a clean card you can cook from.

### Video-only recipes are the weak spot

The trouble starts when the recipe lives only in the video. Lots of cooking reels have a caption like "recipe below" or just a few emojis, and the amounts are said out loud or flashed on screen. Reviewers report that ReciMe's accuracy drops in those cases. You may get a partial ingredient list or steps that skip a detail.

That is not unique to ReciMe, but it matters. If most of your saved reels come from creators who talk through their recipes, you will spend time fixing cards by hand.

### Website and YouTube imports

Because ReciMe also handles recipe websites, it is a good fit if you cook from blogs as much as from reels. It can strip a long blog post down to the recipe itself, which saves a lot of scrolling. Some App Store reviewers have asked for better YouTube support over the years, so test a couple of your own favorite channels during the free trial before you commit.

## What else does ReciMe do?

Beyond import, ReciMe tries to be a full kitchen planner.

### Cookbooks and organization

You can sort recipes into cookbooks, which work like folders. This is handy once you pass a few dozen recipes and want "weeknight dinners" separate from "holiday baking."

### Meal planning

The meal planner lets you drop saved recipes onto days of the week. It is simple and does the job. If you are new to planning, our guide on [how to meal plan for a week](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/) pairs well with any planner, ReciMe's included.

### Grocery lists

ReciMe builds a grocery list from your chosen recipes. That is one of the features users praise most, because it turns a stack of reels into one shopping trip.

### Nutrition info

The listing says ReciMe can calculate calories for recipes. Treat any app's nutrition numbers as estimates, since they depend on how the ingredient was matched and how much you actually use.

## How much does ReciMe cost?

ReciMe is free to download. The free version caps smart imports, and ReciMe Plus removes that cap.

In fall 2026, subscription price trackers listed ReciMe Plus at $39.99 a year in the US, with a free trial. Prices can change and vary by country, so the upgrade screen in the app is the only number you should trust. App Store reviewers are split on value. Some say they happily pay, others call it expensive for what they use.

### Is the free version enough?

For a light user, maybe. If you save one or two recipes a week, the free tier can work. If you save reels every time you scroll, you will likely hit the cap fast and feel nudged toward Plus.

## What are ReciMe's pros and cons?

Here is the honest summary.

### Pros

- Fast, tidy imports when the recipe is in the caption.
- Takes recipes from social media and websites in one place.
- Meal planner and grocery list built in.
- Clean, easy-to-learn layout.
- Works on iPhone and Android.

### Cons

- Weaker on reels where the recipe is only spoken or shown on screen.
- Free tier has a tight import cap.
- Yearly price is higher than some rivals.
- Nutrition numbers are estimates, like every app's.
- Older reviews mention features that changed or were removed, so what you read online may be out of date.

## Who should use ReciMe?

ReciMe suits you if:

- You save recipes from both reels and recipe blogs, and want them in one app.
- Most creators you follow write the recipe in their captions.
- You want meal planning and a grocery list without a second app.
- You use Android, or switch between iPhone and Android.

### Who should pick something else?

Look elsewhere if:

- Your favorite creators talk through recipes instead of writing them down.
- You mostly cook from cookbooks and printed recipes and want to scan pages.
- You want a lower yearly price, or a one-time purchase.
- You only save a few recipes a month and do not want a subscription at all.

## How do you test ReciMe before paying?

A free trial is only useful if you test the right things. Try this during the trial:

1. Pick five reels you actually want to cook. Include at least two where the recipe is only spoken.
2. Import each one and compare the card with the video. Check amounts, not just ingredient names.
3. Add three recipes to the meal plan and build a grocery list. See if the list merges duplicates sensibly.
4. Cook one recipe straight from the app and notice whether you had to touch the screen with messy hands.
5. Set a reminder a day before the trial ends so you can cancel if it is not right.

On iPhone, cancel in Settings, then your name, then Subscriptions.

Nasi does not import from recipe websites, YouTube or Pinterest. For those, you scan a screenshot or paste the recipe text. It also scans photos, cookbook pages and handwritten cards, and it has cookbooks, servings scaling, a weekly meal plan, the Tote shopping list and a hands-free cook mode with timers and voice commands like "next" and "back." It is free with 3 imports a week, and Nasi Pro is $29.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, which works out to $2.50 a month. It is iPhone only. ## Our verdict on ReciMe

ReciMe is a good, well-rounded recipe app. It is strongest for people who pull recipes from both social media and websites, and it earns its keep with the meal planner and grocery list. Its main gap is video-only recipes, and its price is on the higher side. Test it with your own saved reels during the trial, and you will know within a few days whether it fits how you cook.
