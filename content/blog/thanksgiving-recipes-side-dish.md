**The best Thanksgiving side dishes are the ones you can make before Thursday.** Pick 4 to 6 sides, then sort them by how far ahead they can be made. Cranberry sauce and casseroles can be done up to 3 days early, mashed potatoes the day before, and only crisp greens and salads need to wait for the day itself.

Most side dish lists are just long scrolls of photos. This one is sorted the way you actually cook: by when each dish can be made. Use it to build a menu that leaves your oven and your Thursday mostly free. Thanksgiving falls on November 26 this year, so you have plenty of time to plan.

## How many Thanksgiving side dishes should I make?

For 8 to 12 people, plan **4 to 6 sides**. A balanced spread looks like this:

| Role | How many | Why it earns a spot |
|---|---|---|
| Starch | 2 | Mashed potatoes and stuffing are what most guests expect |
| Sweet side | 1 | Sweet potatoes or squash balance the salty dishes |
| Green side | 1 or 2 | Something fresh cuts through the rich plates |
| Cranberry sauce | 1 | Tart, bright and made days ahead |
| Bread | 1 | Rolls or cornbread for the gravy |

If guests offer to bring something, hand them a side. It's the easiest part of the meal to share. For the full menu, including the turkey and desserts, see our guide on [how to plan a Thanksgiving menu](../thanksgiving-recipes-menu/).

## Which Thanksgiving sides can I make 2 to 3 days ahead?

These hold well in the fridge and often taste better after a day or two. Make them the weekend before or on Monday and Tuesday.

### Sauces and relishes

1. **Classic cranberry sauce.** Fresh berries, sugar and orange zest, simmered until they pop. It keeps for about a week.
2. **Cranberry orange relish.** A no-cook version blitzed in a food processor. Bright and crunchy.
3. **Make-ahead gravy base.** Roasted turkey wings or stock thickened with a roux. Whisk in pan drippings on the day.

### Casseroles you assemble now and bake later

4. **Green bean casserole.** Make the creamy mushroom sauce from scratch and fold in blanched beans. Add the crispy onions just before baking.
5. **Sweet potato casserole.** Mash and season the potatoes, spread them in the dish and save the pecan or marshmallow topping for the day.
6. **Baked mac and cheese.** Assemble it fully, then add the breadcrumb topping right before it goes in the oven.
7. **Corn pudding.** A custardy corn bake that reheats beautifully.
8. **Butternut squash gratin.** Thin slices layered with cream, garlic and thyme.

### Breads and baked sides

9. **Cornbread.** Bake it early to cube for dressing, or wrap it whole to warm as a side.
10. **Dinner rolls.** Bake, cool and freeze them. Warm them in foil for 10 minutes before dinner.

**Tip:** label every dish with what it is, the oven temperature and the bake time. On Thursday you won't have to remember anything.

## Which sides are best made the day before?

These need a little more freshness, so Wednesday is their day. Each one reheats without losing much.

11. **Mashed potatoes.** Make them fully, then reheat on the stovetop with a splash of milk and butter. You can also keep them warm in a slow cooker.
12. **Stuffing or dressing.** Assemble it in the baking dish, cover and refrigerate. Bake it while the turkey rests.
13. **Roasted root vegetables.** Carrots, parsnips and beets, roasted until just tender. Reheat them in a hot oven so they crisp again.
14. **Braised red cabbage.** Sweet, sour and better the next day.
15. **Wild rice pilaf.** Cook it with mushrooms and herbs, then add toasted pecans right before serving.
16. **Creamed spinach.** Make the sauce and wilt the spinach, then reheat gently on the stove.
17. **Scalloped potatoes.** Bake them almost all the way, then finish them uncovered so the top browns.
18. **Deviled eggs.** Cook and peel the eggs and make the filling. Fill them on the day.

## What can I cook on Thanksgiving morning?

These are quick to prep, so they suit the morning while the turkey is in the oven. Prep the vegetables on Wednesday to save even more time.

19. **Roasted Brussels sprouts.** Halve and toss them in the morning, then roast at high heat once the turkey comes out.
20. **Honey-glazed carrots.** Simmer them on the stovetop, then add butter and honey at the end.
21. **Maple roasted squash.** Cube and season the squash ahead. It roasts in about 30 minutes.
22. **Garlic green beans.** Blanch them in the morning, then sauté them in garlic butter for 3 minutes before serving.
23. **Glazed sweet potato wedges.** An easier, less sweet option than casserole.
24. **Skillet cornbread.** Mix the dry ingredients the night before. It bakes in about 20 minutes.

## Which sides must be made right before dinner?

Keep this list short, because these all need last-minute attention. Choose one or two at most.

25. **Crisp green salad.** Wash and dry the greens a day ahead. Dress them at the table.
26. **Shaved Brussels sprout salad.** Toss it with lemon, Parmesan and toasted nuts just before serving so it stays crunchy.
27. **Apple and fennel slaw.** Slice everything ahead, store it with a squeeze of lemon and dress it at the last minute.
28. **Pan gravy.** Whisk the turkey drippings into your make-ahead base while the bird rests.
29. **Warm rolls.** Ten minutes in the oven, wrapped in foil.
30. **Fresh herb butter.** Soft butter mashed with chives, parsley and flaky salt for the rolls.

## How do I fit all the sides in one oven?

The oven is the real bottleneck on Thanksgiving. Here's how to fit everything in:

- **Mix your cooking methods.** For every side that needs the oven, pick one that uses the stovetop, slow cooker or no heat at all.
- **Bake during the turkey's rest.** The turkey needs at least 20 minutes out of the oven, and a large bird often rests for 30 to 40. Turn the heat up to 375°F (190°C) and bake your casseroles and stuffing then.
- **Use a slow cooker for mashed potatoes.** It holds them warm for hours and frees a burner.
- **Pick dishes that share a temperature.** Most casseroles bake well at 350°F to 375°F (177°C to 190°C), so they can go in together.

| Cooks in | Sides from this list |
|---|---|
| Oven | Casseroles, stuffing, roasted vegetables, rolls, cornbread |
| Stovetop | Mashed potatoes, gravy, glazed carrots, green beans, creamed spinach |
| Slow cooker | Mashed potatoes, braised red cabbage, corn pudding |
| No-cook | Cranberry relish, salads, slaw, deviled eggs |

## How do I keep make-ahead sides safe?

A few simple rules keep food safe without adding stress:

- Cool cooked dishes quickly and refrigerate them within 2 hours.
- Reheat leftovers and make-ahead dishes until they're hot all the way through, 165°F (74°C).
- After dinner, get leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours and eat them within 3 to 4 days. These follow [USDA food safety guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/).
