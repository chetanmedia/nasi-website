The best potato and leek soup recipes all follow the same simple shape: soften leeks gently in butter, simmer them with potatoes in broth until tender, then blend and season. That's it. Below you'll find a reliable base method, four easy twists that change the whole mood of the pot, and 20 ways to serve it so it never feels like the same dinner twice.

It's cheap, it's cozy, and it's forgiving. If you can chop and stir, you can make a great pot of it tonight.

## What do you need for a classic potato leek soup?

You need very little. Most versions use the same short list, and the quality comes from how gently you cook the leeks.

### The basic ingredients

For about 4 servings, a typical pot uses:

- 2 to 3 medium leeks, white and light green parts only
- 1 1/2 to 2 pounds Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced
- 2 to 3 tablespoons butter (or olive oil)
- 1 to 2 cloves garlic, optional
- 4 cups chicken or vegetable broth
- Salt, black pepper, and a bay leaf or a few thyme sprigs
- A splash of cream or milk, optional

### Which potatoes work best?

Yukon Golds are the favorite for a reason. They blend silky and taste naturally buttery. Russets make a thicker, more rustic soup. Red potatoes hold their shape, so save them for a chunky version you don't blend.

## How do you make potato leek soup step by step?

Here's the general method most cooks use. Treat the timings as guides and taste as you go.

### 1. Clean the leeks properly

Leeks grow in sandy soil, and grit hides between the layers. Trim the root and the tough dark green tops. Slice the leeks in half lengthwise, then into thin half moons. Drop them into a big bowl of cold water, swish, and let the dirt sink. Lift the leeks out instead of pouring them through a strainer, or the grit goes right back on top.

### 2. Sweat, don't brown

Melt the butter in a heavy pot over medium-low heat. Add the leeks with a pinch of salt. Cook them slowly, stirring now and then, for 8 to 10 minutes until soft and sweet. You want them glossy and floppy, not golden. Browning gives a different, slightly bitter flavor. Add the garlic for the last minute.

### 3. Simmer until the potatoes are very tender

Add the potatoes, broth, and herbs. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Cook for 15 to 20 minutes, until a knife slides through a potato cube with no resistance. Fish out the bay leaf or thyme stems.

### 4. Blend carefully

An immersion blender is the easiest and safest option. Blend right in the pot. If you use a countertop blender, work in small batches, fill the jar no more than halfway, remove the center cap from the lid, and cover the hole with a folded towel so steam can escape. Hot soup can push the lid off if it's sealed tight.

One tip: don't over-blend potatoes in a high-speed blender. Too much blending can make them gluey. Pulse until smooth and stop.

### 5. Finish and season

Stir in cream or milk if you like, and warm through without boiling. Taste and adjust the salt. Potato soups usually need more salt than you expect. A squeeze of lemon at the end brightens everything.

## What are 4 easy twists on potato leek soup?

Once you've made the base, these variations take it somewhere new with only a few changes.

### Twist 1: Loaded baked potato style

Keep the soup a little thicker. Top each bowl with shredded cheddar, crumbled crispy bacon, sliced green onions, and a spoon of sour cream. It's the comfort-food version kids tend to love.

### Twist 2: Bright and green

Add a few big handfuls of spinach or chopped kale in the last 2 minutes of simmering, then blend. Finish with lemon zest and plenty of fresh herbs like parsley, dill, or chives. The soup turns a pretty spring green and tastes lighter.

### Twist 3: Smoky and hearty

Brown some sliced smoked sausage or chorizo first, set it aside, and cook the leeks in the same pot. Add a pinch of smoked paprika with the potatoes. Blend only half the soup so it stays chunky, then stir the sausage back in.

### Twist 4: Dairy-free and creamy

Use olive oil instead of butter and vegetable broth instead of chicken. Blend in a splash of unsweetened oat milk or a handful of soaked cashews for body. Nobody misses the cream.

## How do you store and reheat potato leek soup?

Let the soup cool, then refrigerate it within 2 hours in a covered container. It keeps for 3 to 4 days. Reheat portions on the stove over medium heat, stirring often, until steaming hot throughout. The soup thickens in the fridge, so loosen it with a splash of broth or water.

Freezing works, but potatoes can turn a bit grainy. For the best texture, freeze the soup before adding any dairy, use it within 2 to 3 months, and give it a quick blend after reheating if needed.
