You are scrolling, a chilli garlic noodle video stops you cold, and you think "I am making that this week." Three weeks later you are standing in the kitchen scrolling through 400 saved videos trying to remember which one it was.

Every big app has a save button. None of them were built for cooking. This guide covers the built-in way to save recipes on **TikTok**, **Instagram** and **Facebook**, where each one falls short, and a setup that gets you from "ooh" to "dinner" without pausing a video 14 times.

## How to save recipes on TikTok

TikTok's save feature is called **Favorites**.

1. Open the cooking video.
2. Tap the **bookmark icon** on the right-hand side, under the comments icon.
3. To organise, tap **Add to collection** in the pop-up and pick or create a collection like "Dinners".

Find them later in **Profile → the bookmark tab → Videos** or **Collections**.

**Where it falls short:** the recipe still lives inside a video. Amounts are spoken once, flashed on screen for a second or buried in a caption, and you scrub back and forth with greasy fingers to catch them.

## How to save recipes on Instagram

Instagram calls it **Saved**, and it works for Reels and regular posts.

1. Tap the **bookmark icon** under the Reel or post.
2. Tap **Save to collection** to file it somewhere specific.

Find them in **Profile → menu (☰) → Saved**.

**Where it falls short:** many creators put the full recipe in the caption, which is cut off and hard to read while cooking. Others say "recipe in my bio", which sends you to a blog full of ads.

## How to save recipes on Facebook

1. Tap the **three dots (•••)** on the video or post.
2. Choose **Save video** or **Save post**.
3. Optionally add it to a collection.

Find them under **Menu → Saved**.

**Where it falls short:** Facebook's Saved list mixes recipes with everything else you have ever saved, and recipes shared in groups can vanish when a post is removed.

## Why saving the video is not the same as saving the recipe

All three apps save a *pointer to a video*, not the recipe itself. That causes the same problems every time:

| Problem | What happens in the kitchen |
|---|---|
| Amounts are spoken, not written | You replay the clip to hear "two big spoons of chilli crisp" |
| No ingredient list | You cannot shop from it or check what you already have |
| Posts get deleted | Your saved recipe disappears with them |
| No search across apps | Half your recipes are on TikTok, half on Instagram |
| No servings | Cooking for four instead of two means doing the maths yourself |

## The better way: turn the reel into a real recipe

The fix is to get the recipe *out* of the video and into one place where it is written down. You can type it out yourself, or let an app do it.

This is exactly what [Nasi](../../) was built for:

1. In TikTok, Instagram or Facebook, tap **Share** on the cooking video.
2. Pick **Nasi** in the share sheet. That is it, and you can keep scrolling.
3. Nasi watches the video, listens to what the creator says and reads the caption. It writes out the **ingredients with amounts**, **numbered steps**, servings and cook time.
4. The recipe lands in your cookbook with a link back to the original post.

Once it is a real recipe you can scale the servings, add the ingredients to your shopping list, drop it into your meal plan and cook it hands-free.

## Tips for a recipe collection you will actually use

- **Save with intent.** If you would not cook it in the next month, skip it. A smaller collection gets cooked.
- **Sort by when you cook, not by cuisine.** Cookbooks like "Weeknight wins", "Date night" and "Weekend projects" are easier to pick from on a Tuesday.
- **Cook it within a week.** Plan it the day you save it so it does not get buried.
- **Keep the source.** Creators deserve the credit, and the original video is handy for technique.
