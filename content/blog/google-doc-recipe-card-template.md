A Google Docs recipe card template is just a small table with a title row, an ingredients section and a steps section, sized to 4 x 6 or 3 x 5 inches. You can build one in about 10 minutes, save it, and make a copy for every recipe. It's free, it works on any computer or phone, and it's easy to share with the whole family. Here's exactly how to set it up, plus layout ideas, printing tips and a few tricks for collecting recipes from relatives.

## Why use Google Docs for recipe cards?

Google Docs isn't a design tool, and that's the point. It's plain, fast and almost everyone already has it.

### What it does well

- **It's free.** All you need is a Google account.
- **Easy editing.** Fixing a typo or changing "1 cup" to "1 1/4 cups" takes two seconds.
- **Sharing.** You can send a link to aunts, cousins and grandparents and have everyone add recipes to the same folder.
- **Works everywhere.** Edit on a laptop, check a card on your phone in the store.
- **Prints cleanly.** Text-first cards are easy to read in a busy kitchen.

### Where it falls short

If you want illustrated borders, fancy fonts and photo backgrounds, Docs will feel limited. In that case, our guide to the [Canva recipe card template](../canva-recipe-card-template/) is a better fit. Lots of people do both: type in Docs, design in Canva.

## How do you make a recipe card template in Google Docs?

Here's the step-by-step version. Do this once, and you'll never start from a blank page again.

### Step 1: Start a new document

Go to docs.google.com and open a blank document. Name it something clear, like "Recipe Card Template". It's worth peeking at the template gallery (File, then New, then From template) in case a recipe layout is there, but building your own gives you more control.

### Step 2: Set margins

Open File, then Page setup. Set all margins to 0.5 inches. That gives you room to fit two 4 x 6 cards on one letter page. Keep the page on portrait for two cards stacked top to bottom.

### Step 3: Insert a table

Click Insert, then Table, and choose a 1 x 4 grid (one column, four rows). Each row becomes a section of your card:

1. Recipe name
2. Details line (servings, prep time, cook time, from whose kitchen)
3. Ingredients
4. Steps

### Step 4: Size the table to a real card

Right-click the table and open Table properties. Set the column width to 6 inches for a 4 x 6 card, or 5 inches for a 3 x 5 card. Then set row heights so the rows add up to the card height. A good split for a 4 x 6 card:

| Row | 4 x 6 card height | 3 x 5 card height |
|---|---|---|
| Recipe name | 0.5 in | 0.4 in |
| Details line | 0.4 in | 0.3 in |
| Ingredients | 1.5 in | 1.1 in |
| Steps | 1.6 in | 1.2 in |
| **Total** | **4 in** | **3 in** |

Row height in Docs is a minimum, so a row grows if you type too much. That's your cue to use smaller text or move to the back of the card.

### Step 5: Style it

- **Recipe name:** 16 to 18 pt, bold. A friendly font like Lora, Merriweather or Comfortaa looks warm without being hard to read.
- **Details line:** 9 to 10 pt, gray text.
- **Ingredients:** 10 to 11 pt. If the list is long, insert a two-column table inside that cell, or use two side-by-side lists.
- **Steps:** 10 to 11 pt, numbered list.
- **Border:** keep a thin border so you have a cutting line. A light cell shading on the title row adds a nice touch.

### Step 6: Add placeholder text

Fill each section with placeholders, like "Recipe name", "Serves 4 | Prep 15 min | Cook 30 min | From Grandma's kitchen", "1 cup ingredient" and "1. First step". When you make a new card, you just type over them.

### Step 7: Duplicate the card

Copy the whole table and paste it below with a blank line between. Now you have two cards per page. That saves cardstock and makes trimming easy.

## How do you reuse the template for every recipe?

The trick is never to type a recipe into the template itself.

### Make a copy each time

Open your template, click File, then Make a copy, and rename the copy with the recipe name. Your template stays clean forever.

### Keep a recipe folder

Create a folder in Google Drive called "Family Recipes", with subfolders like Mains, Sides, Baking and Holidays. Drop each card in the right place. Drive search finds words inside your docs, so typing "cinnamon" pulls up every card that uses it.

### Or keep one big document

Some families prefer one long document with every card in it, one or two per page. Add a heading above each card (Format, then Paragraph styles, then Heading 2), and the document outline on the left becomes a clickable table of contents. This is also a great base for a printed book. Our guide on how to [make a family recipe book](../make-a-family-recipe-book/) walks through binding and layout.

## What should a recipe card include?

A good card answers every question you'd ask while cooking, so you don't have to guess.

### The essentials

- **Name** of the dish.
- **Servings and times.** Prep, cook and total.
- **Ingredients with exact amounts,** in the order you use them.
- **Numbered steps,** short and clear.
- **Oven temperature and pan size** near the top, so you can preheat early.

### The family touches

- **Whose recipe it is.** "Nani's dal" or "Uncle Rob's chili" means a lot 20 years from now.
- **A short note.** When it was served, or a tip like "use the big pot".
- **Doneness cues.** "Bake until golden and a toothpick comes out clean" beats a time alone, since ovens vary.

If an old recipe says "a cup of flour" with no detail, write down what kind of cup and how you measured it. Our [ingredient substitution chart](../ingredient-substitution-chart/) is handy when an older card calls for something you can't find anymore.

## How do you collect recipes from family with Google Docs?

This is where Docs really shines.

### Share a "make a copy" link

Click Share, set General access to "Anyone with the link" as Viewer, and copy the link. At the end of the link, replace the word "edit" (and anything after it) with "copy". Anyone who opens it gets a prompt to save their own copy of your blank template. They fill it in and share it back.

### Use a shared folder

Share your "Family Recipes" Drive folder with relatives as Editors. Ask everyone to add one card per recipe using the template. Set a gentle deadline, like "before the holidays", if you want a book ready to gift.

### Keep it tidy

- Ask everyone to name files the same way: "Dish name, Person".
- Turn on suggestion mode (the pencil icon at the top right, then Suggesting) when you're editing someone else's card, so they can see your changes.
- Version history (File, then Version history) lets you undo accidental deletions.

## How do you print Google Docs recipe cards?

Printing is where most recipe cards go wrong. A few settings fix it.

### Use cardstock

A 65 to 110 lb cardstock holds up to kitchen splashes. Check your printer's manual for the heaviest paper it takes.

### Print at actual size

Open File, then Print, and make sure scaling is set to 100% or "actual size", not "fit to page". Otherwise your 4 x 6 card shrinks.

### Trim carefully

Cut along the table border with a paper cutter, or a ruler and craft knife. Round the corners with a corner punch if you like a finished look.

### Protect your favorites

Slide the cards you use most into plastic card sleeves. A splash of tomato sauce wipes right off.
