ReciMe vs Recipe Keeper is really a question about where your recipes come from. ReciMe is built to pull recipes out of TikTok, Instagram and other social posts, and it runs on a subscription. Recipe Keeper is an older, steady recipe organizer built around websites and recipes you add yourself, and it works across phones, tablets and computers. If you find dinner in your feed, lean ReciMe. If you cook from blogs and family cards, lean Recipe Keeper.

Below we go feature by feature: imports, organizing, planning, shopping, platforms and price. Then we look at what to use if neither one fits.

## What is the main difference between ReciMe and Recipe Keeper?

They were made for different kitchens.

### ReciMe: built for social media

ReciMe's whole pitch is saving recipes from social posts. You share a TikTok, Instagram or Facebook post to the app, and it builds a recipe card from what it finds. It also takes recipe websites, and it adds a meal planner and grocery list on top. For the full picture, read our [ReciMe review](../recime-review/).

### Recipe Keeper: built for a home recipe box

Recipe Keeper has been around for years as a digital recipe box. You add recipes from websites, type in your own, attach photos, and sort everything into categories. Its strength is keeping one tidy collection on many devices, including a computer, which matters if you like planning at a desk.

### The short version

- Your recipes come from reels: ReciMe fits better.
- Your recipes come from blogs, cookbooks and Grandma: Recipe Keeper fits better.
- You use Android or Windows as well as iPhone: Recipe Keeper covers more ground.

## Which app is better at importing recipes?

Imports are where the two differ most.

### Social video imports

ReciMe wins here, since Recipe Keeper does not try to read cooking videos. ReciMe works best when the creator puts the recipe in the caption. When the recipe is only spoken out loud in the video, results can be thinner, and you may need to fill in amounts yourself. Test it with a few of your own saved reels during the trial.

### Website imports

Both apps can grab a recipe from a web page. Recipe Keeper has done this for a long time and handles it well. ReciMe does it too. If websites are 90% of your cooking, either works, and Recipe Keeper's lower focus on social media will not matter.

### Family recipes and cookbooks

Recipe Keeper is comfortable as a home for typed-in family recipes, with photos and notes. ReciMe can hold them too, but its tools are tuned for imports. If you are digitizing a box of old cards, our guide to [organizing recipes in one place](../recipe-organizer-app-free/) walks through a simple system that works in any app.

## How do they handle organizing and searching?

Both let you build a collection you can actually find things in.

### ReciMe

ReciMe sorts recipes into collections and lets you search what you have saved. It feels like a modern social app, with big photos and a feed-like layout.

### Recipe Keeper

Recipe Keeper uses categories, tags and favorites, and its search works across your whole box. It looks plainer, but it is built for people with hundreds of recipes. Long-time users tend to like that it stays out of the way.

## Which is better for meal planning and grocery lists?

Both include a meal planner and a shopping list, so neither leaves you stranded.

### Planning the week

ReciMe's planner works well if your week is built from recipes you saved off social media. Recipe Keeper's planner works well if your week is built from tried-and-true family dishes. In both, you drop recipes onto days and the plan is there when you shop.

### Building the list

Both can add ingredients from a recipe to a shopping list. Either way, check the list before you shop. Pantry staples like salt and oil often sneak in, and amounts from different recipes do not always combine neatly.

## How much do ReciMe and Recipe Keeper cost?

This is the part people search most, so here is what we could confirm.

### ReciMe

ReciMe is free to download with a cap on smart imports. In fall 2026, price trackers listed ReciMe Plus at $39.99 a year in the US with a free trial. We break the plans down in [Is ReciMe free?](../is-recime-app-free/).

### Recipe Keeper

Recipe Keeper has a free version with limits and a paid upgrade bought inside the app. Its store listings have shown different options over the years, and prices differ by platform and country. We could not confirm one current US price we would stand behind, so open the In-App Purchases section on your store page before you decide.

### Which costs less over time?

It depends on how you buy. A subscription costs the same every year. An upgrade bought per platform can cost more up front if you use several devices, but less over a few years if you stick with one. Count the devices you really cook from.

## What are the pros and cons of each?

### ReciMe pros

- Made for TikTok and Instagram recipes.
- Modern, photo-heavy design.
- Meal planner and grocery list built in.

### ReciMe cons

- Free imports are capped.
- Video-only recipes can come out incomplete.
- No Windows or Mac app.

### Recipe Keeper pros

- Works on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows and Mac.
- Good home for typed-in family recipes.
- Long track record and a plain, fast layout.

### Recipe Keeper cons

- Not built for social video recipes.
- Older look than newer apps.
- Pricing options can be confusing across platforms.

## Who should pick ReciMe, and who should pick Recipe Keeper?

### Pick ReciMe if

- Most of your recipes come from your feed.
- You only cook from an iPhone or Android phone.
- You are fine with a yearly subscription.

### Pick Recipe Keeper if

- You cook mostly from websites, cookbooks and family recipes.
- You want the same collection on a computer.
- You care more about a big, tidy archive than about social imports.

## What if your recipes live in reels the creator only talks through?

That is the gap both apps leave open. Plenty of creators never type the recipe. They say "a good glug of soy sauce" and move on, and caption-based imports miss it.

Nasi was built for exactly this. Share a TikTok, Instagram Reel or Facebook video to Nasi, or paste the link, and it watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption. You get ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator. Nasi does not import recipe websites, so for a blog recipe you can screenshot it and scan it, or paste the text. It is iPhone only, free with 3 imports every week, and Nasi Pro is $29.99 a year with a 3-day free trial.
