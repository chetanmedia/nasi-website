There are **5 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon in 1/3 cup**. Written another way, that is 5 1/3 tablespoons, about 80 mL, or 16 teaspoons. If your 1/3 cup measure is missing, fill a tablespoon five times, add one level teaspoon, and you have it.

Below is a quick chart, the simple math behind it, and a few tricks for halving and doubling.

## What does 1/3 cup equal in tablespoons, teaspoons and ounces?

These are US measures, with metric in brackets. Level every spoon.

| Cups | Tablespoons (tbsp) | Teaspoons (tsp) | Fluid ounces | Milliliters (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/6 cup | 2 tbsp + 2 tsp | 8 tsp | 1 1/3 fl oz | 40 mL |
| 1/4 cup | 4 tbsp | 12 tsp | 2 fl oz | 60 mL |
| 1/3 cup | 5 tbsp + 1 tsp | 16 tsp | 2 2/3 fl oz | 80 mL |
| 1/2 cup | 8 tbsp | 24 tsp | 4 fl oz | 120 mL |
| 2/3 cup | 10 tbsp + 2 tsp | 32 tsp | 5 1/3 fl oz | 160 mL |
| 3/4 cup | 12 tbsp | 36 tsp | 6 fl oz | 180 mL |
| 1 cup | 16 tbsp | 48 tsp | 8 fl oz | 240 mL |

For the quarter-cup version of this chart, see [how many tablespoons in 1/4 cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-1-4-c/). For bigger amounts, [how many tablespoons in a cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-a-cup/) has you covered.

## Why is 1/3 cup 5 tablespoons and 1 teaspoon?

It starts with the cup. One US cup holds 16 tablespoons. Divide 16 by 3 and you get 5 with 1 left over, or 5 1/3 tablespoons.

That leftover third of a tablespoon is exactly 1 teaspoon, because each tablespoon is 3 teaspoons. So 5 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon is the clean, measurable answer.

It is also why thirds feel awkward compared with quarters. A quarter cup divides evenly into 4 tablespoons. A third never does, so you need the teaspoon to finish the job.

### Does it change for dry ingredients?

No. A third of a cup is a volume, so 5 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon works for oil, milk, flour or sugar. What changes is how you fill the spoon:

- **Liquids:** fill to the brim and keep the spoon level.
- **Flour:** spoon it in loosely, then sweep the top flat with a knife. Scooping from the bag packs in too much.
- **Brown sugar:** press it in firmly unless the recipe says otherwise.
- **Butter:** most US sticks are marked in tablespoons, so cut 5 1/3 tablespoons straight from the wrapper.

### What about weight?

Volume and weight are different. A third of a cup of all-purpose flour weighs roughly 40 grams, while a third of a cup of granulated sugar is closer to 67 grams. If a baking recipe lists grams, a kitchen scale gives the most reliable result.

## How do you measure 1/3 cup without a measuring cup?

You have a few simple options.

### Use spoons

Five level tablespoons and one level teaspoon. This is the most accurate method when you have no cup measure.

### Use a liquid measuring jug

Most jugs mark 80 mL or show 1/3 cup on the side. Set the jug on the counter and read it at eye level, not from above.

### Eyeball it for savory cooking

For a soup, stir-fry sauce or marinade, a third of a cup does not need to be perfect. Five tablespoons is close enough, and you can taste and adjust. Save the precision for baking.

## How do you halve or double 1/3 cup?

This is where thirds catch people out.

- **Half of 1/3 cup:** 2 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons (about 40 mL).
- **Double 1/3 cup:** 2/3 cup, or 10 tablespoons plus 2 teaspoons (about 160 mL).
- **Triple 1/3 cup:** exactly 1 cup.
- **1 1/2 times 1/3 cup:** 1/2 cup, or 8 tablespoons.

If you are scaling a whole recipe, convert every ingredient the same way before you start. Writing the new amounts down first saves a lot of mid-recipe math. Our [ingredient substitution chart](../ingredient-substitution-chart/) is handy too if you run out of something partway through.

## Are US and UK tablespoons the same?

Close, but not identical. A US tablespoon is about 14.8 mL, usually rounded to 15 mL. A metric tablespoon used in the UK, Canada and much of Europe is 15 mL. An Australian tablespoon is 20 mL, which is a bigger difference.

The US cup is about 240 mL, while a metric cup is 250 mL. For a third of a cup that gap is only a few milliliters, so it rarely matters outside of careful baking. If a recipe comes from Australia, check whether it uses the 20 mL tablespoon.
