To make a recipe card in Canva, search "recipe card" in the template library, pick a design, replace the text with your recipe, and download it as a PDF Print file. Print it on cardstock at 100% scale and trim. The whole thing takes about 10 minutes per card once your template is set up. Here's the full walkthrough, plus the sizes, layout tips and print settings that make the cards look good in a real recipe box.

## Why make recipe cards in Canva?

Canva is free to start, runs in a browser or on your phone, and handles the design part for you. You don't need to know anything about layouts or fonts.

### What it's good for

- **Gifts.** A set of family recipes on matching cards is a lovely holiday present.
- **Preserving handwritten recipes.** Retype Grandma's cards before the ink fades.
- **A consistent recipe box.** Every card looks the same, so the box is easy to flip through.
- **Recipe books.** One design, many pages, downloaded as one PDF.

If you're building a whole family collection, our guide to [making a family recipe book](../make-a-family-recipe-book/) covers gathering recipes, organizing chapters and printing.

## How do you make a recipe card in Canva, step by step?

Here's the process from a blank screen to a printed card.

### 1. Find a template

Open Canva and type "recipe card" in the search bar. Filter by Templates. You'll see a mix of styles: minimalist, floral, farmhouse, kid-friendly and more. Templates with a crown icon need a paid plan, so skip those if you're on the free version.

### 2. Check the size

Look at the template's dimensions before you edit. If it isn't the size you want, you have two options:

- **Start from a custom size.** Click Create a design, then Custom size, choose inches, and enter 6 x 4 or 5 x 3. Then search the template panel on the left for "recipe card" and apply one.
- **Resize the template.** Canva's resize tool is part of the paid plan. On the free plan, starting from a custom size is easier.

### 3. Set up your master card

Before you add any recipe, decide on your layout and fonts. This becomes the master template for every card.

- **Title** at the top, big and clear.
- **Small info line:** servings, prep time, cook time.
- **Ingredients** in a column on the left or at the top.
- **Steps** numbered, below or to the right.
- **A "from the kitchen of" line** if it's a gift set.

### 4. Add your recipe

Click each text box and replace the placeholder text. Keep ingredient amounts first ("2 cups flour", not "flour, 2 cups") so they're easy to scan while cooking.

### 5. Duplicate for the next recipe

Click the duplicate page button and replace the text. Every card stays consistent, and you only designed it once.

### 6. Download for print

Click Share, then Download, and choose **PDF Print**. Tick "crop marks and bleed" if you're sending it to a print shop. For home printing, the plain PDF Print file is fine.

## What size recipe card should you use?

Pick the size before you design, because changing it later shuffles your layout.

| Size | Inches | Centimeters | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small index card | 3 x 5 | 7.6 x 12.7 | Short recipes, classic recipe boxes |
| Standard recipe card | 4 x 6 | 10.2 x 15.2 | Most recipes, most recipe boxes |
| Half-letter page | 5.5 x 8.5 | 14 x 21.6 | Binders, longer recipes |
| Full letter page | 8.5 x 11 | 21.6 x 27.9 | Recipe books, step photos |

Most people land on 4 x 6. It fits a standard recipe box and gives enough room for an average recipe without tiny text.

## How do you make recipe cards easy to read while cooking?

A pretty card that you can't read across the counter isn't much help. A few rules keep them practical.

### Layout tips

- **Font size of at least 10 to 12 points** for ingredients and steps on a 4 x 6 card.
- **One easy-to-read font for the body.** Save script fonts for the title.
- **High contrast.** Dark text on a light background. Skip text over busy photos.
- **Numbered steps.** It's much easier to find your place.
- **Use both sides** for long recipes. Ingredients on the front, steps on the back.

### Keep a consistent order

Put the same information in the same place on every card. After a few uses, your eyes go straight to the right spot.

## How do you print Canva recipe cards so they last?

Paper choice matters as much as design.

- **Use cardstock.** Something in the 65 to 110 lb range feeds through most home printers and feels sturdy.
- **Print at 100% scale.** "Fit to page" shrinks the card and throws off your trimming.
- **Fit several per page.** Two 4 x 6 cards or three 3 x 5 cards fit on a letter sheet if you lay them out on a letter-size design.
- **Trim with a paper cutter** for clean edges.
- **Protect your favorites.** Card sleeves or laminating keep splashes off the cards you use most.
