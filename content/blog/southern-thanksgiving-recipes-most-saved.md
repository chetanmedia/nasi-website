The Southern Thanksgiving recipes home cooks save again and again are the classics: cornbread dressing, baked mac and cheese, slow-simmered collard greens, candied yams, deviled eggs, giblet gravy, and pecan or sweet potato pie for dessert. They earn their spot because they feed a crowd, many can be made ahead, and they taste like somebody's grandmother made them. Below are 26 of them, grouped by course, with a make-ahead timeline and a few tips most roundups skip.

## What makes a Thanksgiving menu "Southern"?

A Southern Thanksgiving leans on a few things: cornbread instead of white bread in the dressing, plenty of butter and pork for flavor, greens that cook low and slow, and more than one sweet potato dish. The table is usually crowded. Side dishes often outnumber everything else, and nobody apologizes for having two kinds of pie.

### Dressing, not stuffing

Across most of the South, it is dressing, and it bakes in a pan. That matters for safety as well as tradition. Dressing in a pan cooks evenly and crisps on top, and you do not have to worry about the center of a stuffed bird staying underdone.

### The sides are the main event

Ask anyone what they look forward to, and it is rarely the turkey. It is the mac and cheese, the dressing and the greens. Plan your oven and stovetop space around the sides first.

## Which Southern Thanksgiving recipes do people save most?

These are the dishes that show up on nearly every Southern spread. Each line is a general description so you can find a version that suits your family.

### The bird and the gravy

1. **Herb-butter roast turkey.** Butter and herbs under the skin, roasted until the thickest part of the thigh reads 165°F.
2. **Deep-fried turkey.** A Southern favorite for crisp skin, done outdoors only, far from the house, with a thawed and fully dried bird.
3. **Smoked turkey breast.** Easier than a whole bird and frees up the oven.
4. **Giblet gravy.** Pan drippings and broth thickened with a roux, with chopped giblets and sometimes hard-boiled egg.

### Dressing and breads

5. **Classic cornbread dressing.** Crumbled cornbread, sautéed onion and celery, sage, broth and eggs, baked until golden.
6. **Sausage cornbread dressing.** The same base with browned pork sausage folded in.
7. **Oyster dressing.** A coastal tradition, especially along the Gulf and in the Lowcountry.
8. **Buttermilk biscuits.** Tall, flaky and good for mopping up gravy.
9. **Yeast rolls.** Soft dinner rolls brushed with melted butter.
10. **Skillet cornbread.** Baked in a hot cast iron pan for crisp edges.

### Vegetable sides

11. **Collard greens.** Simmered for a long time with smoked turkey or ham hock, onion and a splash of vinegar.
12. **Southern-style green beans.** Cooked low and slow with bacon or ham until soft and full of flavor. For more ideas see our [green bean recipes](../thanksgiving-recipes-green-beans/).
13. **Green bean casserole.** Creamy, topped with crispy fried onions.
14. **Fried okra.** Cornmeal-coated and crunchy.
15. **Black-eyed peas.** Simmered with onion and pork, often saved for New Year's too.
16. **Squash casserole.** Yellow squash baked with cheese and a buttery cracker topping.
17. **Broccoli rice casserole.** Cheesy, creamy and loved by kids.

### Starchy and sweet sides

18. **Baked mac and cheese.** Custard-style with eggs and sharp cheddar, baked until set and browned on top.
19. **Candied yams.** Sweet potatoes simmered or baked in butter, brown sugar and warm spices.
20. **Sweet potato casserole.** Mashed sweet potatoes with a pecan streusel or marshmallow top. Our [sweet potato and yam guide](../yam-thanksgiving-recipes/) has more versions.
21. **Creamy mashed potatoes.** Lots of butter, a little cream, salt to taste.
22. **Cranberry sauce.** Homemade or, in plenty of Southern homes, the canned jellied kind, sliced.

### Starters

23. **Deviled eggs.** Often with a pinch of paprika and a little sweet pickle relish.
24. **Pimento cheese with crackers.** Sharp cheddar, mayo and diced pimentos.

### Desserts

25. **Pecan pie.** Rich, gooey filling with toasted pecans, sometimes with a splash of bourbon.
26. **Sweet potato pie.** Smooth, lightly spiced and a little less sweet than pecan. Banana pudding with vanilla wafers is a common bonus dessert.

## How do I make a Southern Thanksgiving ahead of time?

A big Southern spread is a lot of dishes. The trick is to spread the work across the week so Thursday is mostly reheating and baking.

### Make-ahead timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 week before | Make your menu, check pan sizes and write the grocery list |
| 4 to 5 days before | Start thawing a frozen turkey in the fridge (about 1 day per 4 to 5 lb) |
| 3 days before | Bake the cornbread for dressing and let it dry out, covered loosely |
| 2 days before | Make cranberry sauce, pimento cheese and pie dough |
| 1 day before | Bake pies, boil eggs, cook collards, assemble dressing, mac and cheese and casseroles |
| Thanksgiving morning | Roast the turkey, fill deviled eggs, bring casseroles to the counter for 30 minutes |
| While the turkey rests | Bake dressing and casseroles, make gravy, warm greens and rolls |

Collard greens and many braised green beans taste even better the next day, so cooking them early is a win twice.

### Free up the oven

The oven is always the bottleneck. Greens, black-eyed peas and candied yams can all live on the stovetop. A slow cooker can hold mashed potatoes or mac and cheese warm for hours. ## How much food do I need for a Southern Thanksgiving?

Southern tables run generous, and leftovers are part of the plan. A few rough guides:

- **Turkey:** about 1 to 1.5 pounds per person for a whole bird, which covers bones and a little leftover.
- **Dressing:** one 9x13 pan feeds about 10 to 12 as a side.
- **Mac and cheese:** one 9x13 pan feeds about 10 to 12 alongside everything else.
- **Pies:** one pie per 6 to 8 guests, plus an extra if you want leftovers.

If you need to size the bird exactly, the [turkey calculator](../../tools/turkey-calculator/) does the math. To scale a family recipe up or down, try the [recipe scaler](../../tools/recipe-scaler/).

### Keep leftovers safe

Do not let food sit out for more than 2 hours. Pack leftovers into shallow containers, refrigerate them, and eat them within 3 to 4 days. Reheat to 165°F. Pull the turkey meat off the bone before storing so it chills quickly.
