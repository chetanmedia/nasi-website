There are three ways to save recipes on TikTok: tap the bookmark icon to add the video to your Favorites, sort those favorites into Collections, or turn the video into a written recipe that lives outside the app. The first two take a second but break if the creator deletes the post. The third takes a little longer and never breaks, because you keep the ingredients and steps yourself.

Here is how each one works, when to use it, and how to stop losing the recipes you actually want to cook.

## How do you save a recipe video on TikTok?

The quickest way is the bookmark. It is built into every video and takes one tap.

### Method 1: Add it to Favorites

1. Open the recipe video.
2. Tap the bookmark icon on the right side of the screen, under the comment button.
3. The icon turns yellow. The video is now saved.

To find it later, go to your profile and tap the bookmark tab. Every video you have saved lives there, newest first.

This is fine for a recipe you plan to make this week. It is not great for a recipe you want next Thanksgiving. After a few months, your Favorites fill up with dance clips, travel tips and fifty pasta videos, and scrolling through them is slow.

### Method 2: Sort recipes into Collections

Collections are folders inside your Favorites. They turn a messy pile into something you can browse.

1. Go to your profile and open the bookmark tab.
2. Tap Collections, then the option to create a new one.
3. Give it a clear name, like "Weeknight dinners", "Baking" or "Holiday sides".
4. Pick the saved videos you want to add, then save.

Next time you bookmark a recipe, TikTok will often show a small prompt to add it to a collection right away. Use it. Sorting as you save takes two seconds. Sorting 300 videos later takes an afternoon.

A few naming ideas that work well:

- By meal: breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks
- By time: under 20 minutes, weekend projects
- By diet: vegetarian, high protein, dairy free
- By occasion: Thanksgiving, birthdays, potlucks

You can keep a collection private or share it with friends, which is handy if your household plans meals together.

## Why do saved TikTok recipes stop working?

Bookmarks and Collections save a link to the video, not the recipe. That difference matters more than it seems.

### The video can vanish

If the creator deletes the post, makes their account private, or the video gets taken down, it disappears from your Favorites too. You get no warning. You just open your "Dinner" collection one evening and find a gap.

### The recipe is often not written down

Many TikTok recipes live only in the video. The creator says "a splash of soy sauce" and "about a cup of rice" while their hands move fast. The caption might list the dish name and a few hashtags, nothing more. Some creators put the full recipe in the comments, a linked page or a bio link, and some do not post it anywhere.

### Searching is hard

You cannot search inside your Favorites by ingredient. If you remember "that chicken thing with lemon and orzo", you scroll and hope.

So the bookmark is a good "maybe later" button. It is a poor recipe box.

## How do you turn a TikTok video into a written recipe?

This is the method that never breaks. You end up with ingredients, amounts and steps saved somewhere you control, plus a link back to the creator so they still get credit.

### Method 3a: Write it down yourself

The free, manual way:

1. Watch the video once without stopping, just to understand the dish.
2. Watch again and pause at each step. Note every ingredient and amount you see or hear.
3. Check the caption and pinned comments for anything you missed.
4. Write it out in your notes app, a doc or a paper card: title, servings, ingredients, then numbered steps.
5. Paste the video link at the top so you can find the creator again.

This works, but expect 10 to 15 minutes per recipe. Spoken amounts are easy to mishear, and "season to taste" leaves you guessing. If an amount is unclear, start small and adjust as you cook.

### Method 3b: Let an app write it for you

A recipe app built for social video can do the same job in under a minute. You share the TikTok to the app, and it gives you back a clean recipe card.

What to look for:

- **It reads the video, not just the caption.** Most of the recipe is often spoken, so an app that only reads text will miss a lot.
- **Amounts and steps.** You want "2 tbsp soy sauce", not just "soy sauce".
- **A link to the creator.** Good manners, and handy if you want to rewatch a technique.
- **Search.** The whole point is finding the recipe later.

## Which way to save TikTok recipes is best?

It depends on how likely you are to cook the dish. Here is a quick way to decide.

| Method | Time per recipe | Survives if video is deleted | Searchable by ingredient | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Favorites (bookmark) | 1 second | No | No | Maybe-later ideas |
| Collections | 2 to 5 seconds | No | No | Browsing by theme |
| Writing it by hand | 10 to 15 minutes | Yes | Depends where you save it | One or two keepers |
| Recipe app import | Under a minute | Yes | Yes | Anything you will actually cook |

A simple habit that works: bookmark freely while you scroll, then once a week go through your saves. Anything you still want to cook gets turned into a written recipe. Anything else can stay as a bookmark or be removed.

## How do you keep TikTok recipes organized once you save them?

Saving is half the job. The other half is being able to find and use the recipe on a busy Tuesday.

### Use the same categories everywhere

If your TikTok collections are called "Dinners" and "Baking", use the same names wherever you keep written recipes. Your brain only has to learn one system.

### Add a note while it is fresh

Right after you save, add one line: "Kids loved it", "Needs more garlic", "Good for meal prep". Future you will thank you.

### Plan with what you saved

Saved recipes are only useful if you cook them. Pick two or three from your saves each week and build your plan around them. Our guide on [how to meal plan for a week](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/) walks through a simple routine.

## Can you save a TikTok recipe without the app?

Yes, a few ways:

- **Copy the link.** Tap Share, then Copy link, and paste it into your notes. You can open it in a browser later. It still breaks if the video is deleted.
- **Download the video.** Some creators allow downloads. Tap Share, then Save video. The file lands in your camera roll. It takes up storage and is not searchable, and not every video allows it.
- **Screenshot the key moments.** Quick, but a list of screenshots is hard to cook from.

All three keep the video or a pointer to it. None gives you a clean list of ingredients and steps.
