When you're staring into the fridge wondering what to make with the ingredients you have, pick a forgiving format first, then fill it with what's on hand. Stir-fries, frittatas, fried rice, soups, pasta, tacos and grain bowls work with almost any mix of protein, vegetables and pantry staples. Below are 28 flexible dinner ideas grouped by how much time you have, plus a simple method for turning odds and ends into a real meal.

No shopping trip needed. Just a quick look at what you've got.

## How do I figure out what to cook with what I have?

The trick is to stop looking for a perfect recipe and start thinking in templates. A template is a dish shape that welcomes swaps.

### Step 1: Find what needs using first

Check for the things that spoil fastest. Half a bag of spinach, a chicken breast near its date, a lonely zucchini. That's your starting point.

### Step 2: Pick a format

Ask yourself one question: do I want it in a pan, a pot, the oven or a bowl? That narrows things fast.

- **Pan:** stir-fry, fried rice, quesadillas, hash, frittata
- **Pot:** soup, chili, curry, pasta
- **Oven:** sheet pan dinner, baked pasta, roasted vegetables
- **Bowl:** grain bowl, noodle salad, rice bowl

### Step 3: Add a flavor direction

Same ingredients, very different dinners. Soy sauce, garlic and ginger take you one way. Cumin, chili and lime take you another. Tomato, garlic and oregano another. Pick one and commit.

### Step 4: Finish with something fresh or crunchy

A squeeze of lemon, a handful of herbs, toasted nuts, a fried egg, a spoon of yogurt. It's the step that makes leftovers taste like a meal.

## What can I make in 15 minutes with what I have?

These are the "it's late and I'm hungry" dinners. Most only need a pan.

1. **Fried rice.** Day-old rice, any vegetables, an egg or two, soy sauce. Better with cold rice than fresh.
2. **Quesadillas.** Tortillas, cheese and whatever is lurking: beans, leftover chicken, peppers, corn.
3. **Garlic butter pasta.** Pasta, garlic, butter or oil, and cheese if you have it. Add frozen peas or spinach.
4. **Egg fried noodles.** Instant or dried noodles, eggs, a few vegetables and any savory sauce.
5. **Loaded toast.** Good bread with beans, eggs, sautéed greens, tomatoes or cheese. Dinner, not just breakfast.
6. **Tuna or chickpea salad wraps.** Canned protein, mayo or yogurt, something crunchy, wrapped up.
7. **Shakshuka-style eggs.** Eggs poached in canned tomatoes with onion, garlic and spices.

## What can I make in 30 minutes from the fridge?

A little more time opens up soups, curries and bowls.

8. **Clean-out-the-fridge stir-fry.** Slice everything thin, cook the hardest vegetables first, finish with a quick sauce.
9. **Frittata.** Eggs plus any cooked vegetables, potatoes, cheese or meat. Starts on the stove, finishes in the oven.
10. **Bean and vegetable soup.** Onion, garlic, broth, canned beans, any vegetables, simmered until tender.
11. **Chickpea curry.** Canned chickpeas, canned tomatoes or coconut milk, curry powder or paste. Serve with rice.
12. **Grain bowls.** Rice or any grain, a protein, roasted or raw vegetables, and a sauce you can whisk in a minute.
13. **Pasta with greens and beans.** Pasta, canned white beans, garlic, any greens and a little pasta water to bring it together.
14. **Tacos with anything.** Season ground meat, beans, eggs or roasted vegetables with cumin and chili. Tortillas do the rest.
15. **Peanut noodles.** Peanut butter, soy sauce, something sour and something sweet, tossed with noodles and crunchy vegetables.
16. **Potato hash.** Diced potatoes crisped in a pan with onions, peppers and leftover meat. Top with an egg.

## What can I make if I have more time?

Got an hour and some patience? These turn cheap staples into something cozy.

17. **Sheet pan dinner.** Chop vegetables and a protein to similar sizes, toss with oil and spices, roast at 425°F.
18. **Baked pasta.** Pasta, sauce, any cooked vegetables or meat, and cheese on top. Bake until bubbling.
19. **Chili.** Ground meat or extra beans, canned tomatoes, onion and chili spices. The longer it simmers, the better.
20. **Stuffed peppers or squash.** Fill with rice, beans or meat, tomato sauce and cheese.
21. **Lentil stew.** Dried lentils, onion, carrot, celery and broth. Very forgiving and very cheap.
22. **Roast chicken and vegetables.** Chicken pieces on a bed of root vegetables and onions. One pan.
23. **Homemade pizza or flatbread.** Store-bought dough or flatbread with whatever toppings need using up.

## What can I make with mostly pantry staples?

When the fridge is nearly empty, the pantry still has dinner in it.

24. **Pasta e fagioli.** Pasta and canned beans in a tomato broth. Hearty, and almost entirely shelf-stable.
25. **Rice and beans.** Season well with onion, garlic and cumin. Add a fried egg or salsa on top.
26. **Coconut lentil dal.** Red lentils, canned coconut milk and spices. Cooks in about 25 minutes.
27. **Pantry tomato soup.** Canned tomatoes, onion, garlic and broth, blended. Grilled cheese optional but encouraged.
28. **Tuna pasta.** Canned tuna, pasta, olive oil, lemon or capers, and any canned vegetables.

## Which pantry staples make "what's for dinner" easier?

If you keep a small set of staples stocked, you can almost always pull together one of the meals above.

### The basics

- Rice, pasta and one other grain or noodle
- Canned beans, chickpeas and lentils
- Canned tomatoes and a carton of broth
- Eggs, onions and garlic
- Frozen vegetables (peas, spinach, mixed vegetables)

### The flavor shelf

- Soy sauce and a vinegar
- A curry paste or powder
- Cumin, chili powder and dried oregano
- Peanut butter
- Lemons or limes

## How do I use up leftovers without wasting food?

Cooking from what you have is also the easiest way to waste less.

### Keep a "use first" spot

Put the items that need using up on one shelf at eye level. You'll see them every time you open the door.

### Repurpose instead of reheat

Leftover roast chicken becomes fried rice, tacos or soup. Extra rice becomes fried rice or rice bowls. Roasted vegetables go into a frittata or blend into soup.

### Follow basic food safety

Refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours. Most cooked leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge. Reheat them until steaming hot, 165°F in the center. When in doubt, throw it out.

### Plan one "fridge clean-out" night

Pick one night a week, often Thursday or Friday, for using up what's left. It takes the pressure off the rest of the week. ## Quick recap

- Start with what spoils first.
- Pick a forgiving format: pan, pot, oven or bowl.
- Choose one flavor direction and commit.
- Finish with something fresh, sour or crunchy.
- Keep a short list of pantry staples stocked.

That's really all it takes. Open the fridge, pick a template, and dinner is closer than you think.
