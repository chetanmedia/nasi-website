The easiest high-protein meal prep ideas are built around one or two proteins you cook in bulk, like chicken thighs, ground turkey, eggs, tofu or lentils, then mix into different bowls, wraps and breakfasts all week. Cook the protein, a grain and a tray of vegetables on Sunday, keep a couple of high-protein add-ons on hand, and you can build a filling meal in five minutes. Below are ideas for every meal, a one-session plan, and how long everything keeps.

## What makes meal prep high in protein?

A high-protein prep is not about special recipes. It is about making protein the base of each container, then adding carbs, vegetables and sauce around it.

### Start with a protein anchor

Pick one or two of these to cook in bulk each week:

- **Chicken thighs or breasts.** Thighs stay juicier when reheated.
- **Ground turkey, chicken or lean beef.** Fast to brown and easy to season different ways.
- **Eggs.** Boiled, baked into muffins, or scrambled into burritos.
- **Salmon or other fish.** Great early in the week.
- **Tofu or tempeh.** Bake or pan-fry so it holds its shape.
- **Lentils and beans.** Cheap, filling and good in soups, salads and bowls.

### Add a high-protein extra

Small add-ons push a meal higher without more cooking: Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, edamame, chickpeas, cheese, hemp hearts, nuts and seeds. A spoon of Greek yogurt in a sauce or a scoop of cottage cheese in eggs adds up fast.

## What are good high-protein breakfast meal prep ideas?

Breakfast is where protein is easiest to miss. These hold up for several days. For more morning ideas, see our full list of [breakfast meal prep ideas](../meal-prep-ideas-for-breakfast/).

### Make-ahead breakfasts

- **Egg muffins.** Whisk eggs with cottage cheese, spinach and diced peppers, bake in a muffin tin, cool and refrigerate.
- **Breakfast burritos.** Scrambled eggs, black beans, cheese and salsa rolled in tortillas. They freeze well.
- **Greek yogurt parfait jars.** Layer yogurt with berries, keep granola separate so it stays crunchy.
- **Protein overnight oats.** Oats soaked in milk and Greek yogurt, topped with nut butter in the morning.
- **Hard-boiled eggs.** Boil a dozen on Sunday for the fastest grab-and-go option.
- **Cottage cheese bowls.** Portion cottage cheese with fruit or with cucumber and everything bagel seasoning.

## What are the best high-protein lunch prep ideas?

Lunches need to taste good cold or after a quick reheat. Bowls and salads with a sturdy base work best.

### Bowls and salads

- **Chicken and rice bowls.** Sliced chicken thighs, rice, roasted broccoli and a yogurt-based sauce.
- **Turkey taco bowls.** Seasoned ground turkey, black beans, corn, rice and salsa.
- **Chickpea and tuna salad.** Canned tuna, chickpeas, red onion, lemon and olive oil. No cooking needed.
- **Lentil salad.** Cooked lentils, feta, cucumber, tomato and a mustard vinaigrette.
- **Tofu peanut noodle bowls.** Baked tofu, noodles, shredded cabbage and a peanut sauce kept on the side.
- **Greek chicken salad jars.** Chicken, chickpeas, cucumber and feta, with dressing at the bottom and greens on top.

### Wraps and snack boxes

- **Chicken Caesar wraps.** Use a Greek yogurt Caesar dressing to add protein.
- **Protein snack boxes.** Boiled eggs, cheese cubes, deli turkey, edamame, nuts and fruit.

## What are easy high-protein dinner prep ideas?

Dinners can be a little heartier. Pick one batch-cooked main and serve it two ways.

### Batch-cooked mains

- **Turkey meatballs in marinara.** Serve with pasta one night and in a sub roll the next.
- **Sheet-pan salmon and vegetables.** Eat it in the first two or three days.
- **Beef and broccoli.** Lean beef strips with broccoli and a soy-ginger sauce over rice.
- **Chicken chili with beans.** Ground chicken, two kinds of beans and tomatoes. It gets better by day two.
- **Lentil and sausage soup.** Lentils and a little sausage go a long way.
- **Baked tofu stir-fry.** Crisp tofu with mixed vegetables and a sauce you add when reheating.
- **Shredded chicken.** Slow-cook chicken with salsa, then use it in tacos, bowls and quesadillas.

## How do you meal prep high-protein meals in one session?

You can prep a full week of high-protein food in about two hours. Here is a simple order that keeps the oven and stove busy at the same time.

### A simple Sunday plan

1. **Preheat the oven.** Put chicken thighs on one sheet pan and chopped vegetables on another.
2. **Start the grain.** Rice, quinoa or farro on the stove or in a rice cooker.
3. **Brown ground meat.** Season half for tacos and half for an Italian-style sauce.
4. **Boil eggs.** Cool them in ice water, then refrigerate.
5. **Make one sauce.** A yogurt herb sauce or peanut sauce, stored in a jar.
6. **Cool, then portion.** Let food cool before lidding, and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking.

## How long does high-protein meal prep last?

Food safety matters more with meat, fish and eggs. Use these standard fridge times, with the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or below.

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked chicken or turkey | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 6 months |
| Cooked ground meat | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Cooked fish | 3 to 4 days (best in 2 to 3) | 2 to 3 months |
| Hard-boiled eggs (in shell) | Up to 1 week | Not recommended |
| Cooked beans and lentils | 3 to 5 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Baked tofu | 4 to 5 days | Up to 3 months (texture changes) |

### Tips for safe reheating

- Reheat leftovers until steaming hot all the way through, 165°F (74°C).
- Thaw frozen meals in the fridge, not on the counter.
- Prep two to three days at a time and freeze the rest if you want a full five-day week.
