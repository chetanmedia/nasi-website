The best recipe app is the one that matches where your recipes actually come from. If you save most of them from TikTok, Instagram Reels or Facebook videos, you want an app that turns a video into a written recipe. If you mostly clip from recipe websites, an established organizer like Paprika or Copy Me That does that job well. Below, we compare seven popular options on price, import sources and cooking features, then tell you who should pick what.

## How did we choose and compare these recipe apps?

We looked at each app the way a home cook actually uses one: you find a recipe somewhere, you save it, and a week later you need to cook from it.

### What we compared

- **Import sources.** Can it save from social video, recipe websites, photos or PDFs?
- **Recipe quality.** Do you get clean ingredients and steps, or a messy copy?
- **Cooking features.** Scaling, shopping lists, meal planning and a cook mode.
- **Price.** Free tier, one-time purchase or subscription.
- **Platforms.** iPhone only, or iPhone plus Android, web and desktop.

### A note on prices

Prices change, and they vary by country. We list a number only where we could confirm it from the app's own site or store listing. Where we could not confirm a current price, we say so. Always check the paywall in the app before you subscribe.

## Which recipe app is best for saving from TikTok and Instagram?

This is where most "best recipe app" lists fall short. They were written when recipes lived on blogs. Today, a huge share of the recipes people actually cook start as a 60-second video, often with no written recipe at all.

### Why video recipes are hard to save

A cooking reel might list the ingredients only on screen, or only out loud. Amounts are often spoken ("about a tablespoon of honey") and never written down. Bookmarking the video inside TikTok or Instagram saves the clip, not the recipe, and creators sometimes delete posts. We cover the details in our guide to [saving recipes from TikTok](../how-to-save-recipes-on-tiktok/).

### Nasi

Nasi is built for exactly this. Share a TikTok, Instagram Reel or Facebook video to Nasi (or paste the link), and it watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption. Then it writes out the ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator.

It also scans photos, screenshots, cookbook pages and handwritten cards, takes a PDF (one recipe per PDF), or lets you paste text or write a recipe by hand. Once saved, you get searchable cookbooks and favorites, servings scaling, a shopping list (the Tote), a weekly meal plan and hands-free cook mode with timers and voice commands.

**Good to know:** Nasi does not import from recipe website links, YouTube or Pinterest. For those, the workaround is to screenshot the recipe and scan it, or copy the text and paste it in. It is also iPhone only.

**Price:** Free with 3 imports every week. Nasi Pro is $29.99 a year with a 3-day free trial, and adds unlimited imports, smart scanning, cook mode, and sync and backup.

### ReciMe

ReciMe is one of the best known apps for saving recipes from social media. It imports from social platforms and from recipe websites, and includes meal planning and grocery lists. It runs on both iPhone and Android, which matters if your household is mixed.

**Price:** There is a free tier and a paid subscription. Published prices for the yearly plan vary by region and source, so check the in-app paywall for your current price.

**Pick it if:** you want one app for both social and web recipes, or you need Android.

## Which recipe app is best for recipe websites?

If your recipes mostly come from food blogs and big recipe sites, the older organizers are still excellent.

### Paprika

Paprika has been a favorite with serious home cooks for years. It has a built-in browser: open a recipe page, tap a button, and it pulls the recipe out of the clutter. It handles meal planning, grocery lists, scaling and pantry tracking, and it stores recipes on your device so you can cook offline.

**Price:** A one-time purchase, sold separately for each platform (mobile and desktop). On the US App Store the iPhone and iPad version has been listed at $4.99, but check your store, since prices vary by region and change during sales.

**Pick it if:** you clip from websites, hate subscriptions and want something that works without a connection.

### Copy Me That

Copy Me That is a simple, affordable clipper. A browser extension and app let you save recipes from websites, then organize, plan meals and build shopping lists.

**Price:** A free tier, plus Premium at $12.99 a year or $24.99 for lifetime access, according to the Copy Me That premium page.

**Pick it if:** you want the cheapest reliable way to clip web recipes and do not mind a plainer design.

## Which recipe app is best as a long-term recipe box?

Some people want less of a discovery app and more of a permanent home for every recipe they love, including family ones.

### Recipe Keeper

Recipe Keeper imports from websites, lets you type recipes in or add your own photos, and organizes them into categories with meal planning and shopping lists. It runs on almost every platform, including Windows and Mac.

**Price:** A free version, plus a one-time Pro upgrade. Third-party price trackers list the iPhone upgrade at around $19.99, but confirm the price in your store.

**Pick it if:** you want a long-term archive across many devices and prefer paying once.

### A note on family recipes

Handwritten cards are the recipes people most regret losing. Any app on this list that scans photos can help, but it is worth testing with your messiest card before you commit. Nasi, for example, can scan a card into an editable recipe.

## Which recipe app is best for meal planning?

If your main goal is deciding what to cook this week, look at apps built around planning.

### Samsung Food

Samsung Food (which grew out of Whisk) is free to use for saving recipes, building meal plans and making shopping lists, with a large community recipe library. A paid Food+ tier adds AI meal plans and extra features. Some features work best with a Samsung account or Samsung appliances.

**Price:** Free, with a paid Food+ subscription. Check the app for current pricing in your region.

**Pick it if:** you want strong free meal planning and like browsing new recipes.

### Mealime

Mealime is less a recipe saver and more a meal-planning service. You pick your diet and preferences, choose from its own quick, healthy recipes, and it builds a grocery list. The Pro tier adds extras like nutrition information and more recipes.

**Price:** Free, with a paid Pro tier. Mealime has changed its pricing over time, so check the upgrade screen for current plans.

**Pick it if:** you want someone else to plan dinner, not a place to store recipes you found.

For a planning method that works with any app, see our guide on [how to meal plan for a week](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/).

## What happened to Yummly?

Yummly, once one of the most popular recipe apps, shut down its website and apps in December 2024. If you still have an old export or screenshots of your Yummly favorites, you can rebuild your collection in any app here. A screenshot scan is often the fastest way to bring those recipes back.

## What is the best free recipe app?

Most apps on this list have a useful free tier. The right one depends on how many recipes you save:

- **Saving a few video recipes a week:** Nasi's free plan gives you 3 imports every week, plus scanning and manual recipes.
- **Clipping lots of web recipes on a budget:** Copy Me That's free tier, then Premium if you outgrow it.
- **Planning meals for free:** Samsung Food's free plan is generous.
- **Trying before buying a one-time app:** Recipe Keeper's free version lets you test it with a handful of recipes.

Whatever you pick, the same rules apply: choose based on where your recipes come from, and favor an app that lets you get your recipes back out if you ever switch.

## How do you pick the right recipe app for you?

Ask yourself three questions.

### 1. Where do your recipes come from?

Look at your last ten saved recipes. If most are videos on TikTok or Instagram, a video-first app saves you the most time. If most are blog links, a web clipper fits better. Mixed? Choose the app that handles your most common source best, and use screenshots or pasted text for the rest.

### 2. What device do you use?

Some apps are iPhone only (Nasi), and some run everywhere (Paprika, Recipe Keeper, Samsung Food). If you cook from a Windows laptop or an Android tablet, that decides it.

### 3. Do you want to pay once or yearly?

One-time apps like Paprika and Recipe Keeper suit people who rarely change tools. Subscriptions usually include cloud sync and ongoing features like video imports, which cost money to run every time you use them.

### Test with real recipes

Install your top two. Save the same five recipes in each: one video, one website, one screenshot, one handwritten card and one you type in. Then cook one meal from each app. You will know within a weekend.
