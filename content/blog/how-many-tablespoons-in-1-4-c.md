There are **4 tablespoons in 1/4 cup**. That is about 60 mL, or 2 fluid ounces, and the same 4 tablespoons equal 12 teaspoons. If your quarter cup measure is missing or dirty, fill a tablespoon four times, level each spoon, and you have it.

The rest of this page is a quick chart and a few tricks for the odd amounts that show up when you halve or double a recipe.

## What does 1/4 cup equal in tablespoons, teaspoons and ounces?

These are US measures, with metric in brackets. Level every spoon.

| Cups | Tablespoons (tbsp) | Teaspoons (tsp) | Fluid ounces | Milliliters (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/16 cup | 1 tbsp | 3 tsp | 1/2 fl oz | 15 mL |
| 1/8 cup | 2 tbsp | 6 tsp | 1 fl oz | 30 mL |
| 1/4 cup | 4 tbsp | 12 tsp | 2 fl oz | 60 mL |
| 1/3 cup | 5 tbsp + 1 tsp | 16 tsp | 2 2/3 fl oz | 80 mL |
| 3/8 cup | 6 tbsp | 18 tsp | 3 fl oz | 90 mL |
| 1/2 cup | 8 tbsp | 24 tsp | 4 fl oz | 120 mL |
| 3/4 cup | 12 tbsp | 36 tsp | 6 fl oz | 180 mL |
| 1 cup | 16 tbsp | 48 tsp | 8 fl oz | 240 mL |

## Why is 1/4 cup exactly 4 tablespoons?

It comes straight from the cup. One US cup holds 16 tablespoons. A quarter of 16 is 4. That is all there is to it.

The same math gives you the teaspoons. Each tablespoon is 3 teaspoons, so 4 tablespoons is 12 teaspoons. If you want the full picture of spoon math, our guide to [teaspoons and tablespoons](../teaspoon-is-how-many-tablespoons/) covers every common swap, and [how many tablespoons in a cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-a-cup/) walks through the larger amounts.

### Does it change for dry ingredients?

No. A quarter cup is a volume, so 4 tablespoons works for flour, sugar, oil or milk. What changes is how you fill the spoon:

- **Liquids:** fill to the brim and hold the spoon level so nothing spills.
- **Flour:** spoon it in loosely, then sweep the top flat with a knife. Do not scoop straight from the bag, or you will pack in extra.
- **Brown sugar:** press it in firmly, unless the recipe says otherwise.

### What about weight?

Volume and weight are not the same thing. A quarter cup of all-purpose flour weighs roughly 30 grams, while a quarter cup of granulated sugar is closer to 50 grams. If a baking recipe gives grams, use a scale for the best result.

## How do you measure 1/4 cup without a measuring cup?

You have a few easy options.

### Use a tablespoon four times

This is the most accurate fallback. Four level tablespoons is a quarter cup, full stop. Count out loud if you are distracted. It is easy to lose track at three.

### Use a liquid measuring jug

Most jugs have a 2 fl oz or 60 mL line. That is your quarter cup. Read it at eye level on a flat counter.

### Use butter wrapper marks

A standard US stick of butter is 8 tablespoons, or 1/2 cup. Cut it at the 4 tablespoon line and you have 1/4 cup of butter.

### Use a shot glass, carefully

Many shot glasses hold about 1.5 fl oz, which is less than a quarter cup. Only use one if you know its size. When in doubt, go back to the tablespoon.

## How do you halve or double 1/4 cup?

This is where most mix-ups happen. Keep these handy:

- **Half of 1/4 cup** is 1/8 cup, which is 2 tablespoons.
- **A quarter of 1/4 cup** is 1 tablespoon.
- **Double 1/4 cup** is 1/2 cup, or 8 tablespoons (more in [how many tablespoons in 1/2 cup](../how-many-tablespoons-is-1-2-cup/)).
- **Triple 1/4 cup** is 3/4 cup, or 12 tablespoons.
- **1 1/2 times 1/4 cup** is 6 tablespoons, or 3/8 cup.

If a recipe calls for 1/4 cup of something and you are making a third of the batch, it is easier to think in spoons. A third of 12 teaspoons is 4 teaspoons, which is 1 tablespoon plus 1 teaspoon.

### What about British and Australian cups?

A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand and Canada, is 250 mL, so a quarter cup there is about 62.5 mL. That is close enough to the US 60 mL for almost any home cooking. The bigger trap is the Australian tablespoon, which is 20 mL rather than 15 mL. That means an Australian quarter cup is only a little over 3 of their tablespoons. If a recipe comes from Australia, use their cup measure rather than counting spoons.

## Which ingredients usually come in 1/4 cup amounts?

A quarter cup is one of the most common measures in home cooking. You will see it for:

- Oil or melted butter in muffins and quick breads
- Soy sauce, honey or vinegar in marinades and stir-fry sauces
- Chopped herbs, green onions or nuts as a garnish
- Grated parmesan stirred into pasta or soup
- Milk or cream to loosen mashed potatoes
