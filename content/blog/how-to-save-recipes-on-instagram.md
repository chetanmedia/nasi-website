There are three ways to save recipes on Instagram: tap the bookmark icon to add the Reel to your Saved list, sort those saves into collections, or turn the Reel into a written recipe that lives outside the app. The first two take a second, but they only save the video. The third gives you the ingredients and steps, so you can actually cook from it and keep it even if the post disappears.

Here's how each one works, and how to stop scrolling through a 40-second video with flour on your hands.

## How do you save a recipe Reel on Instagram?

The quickest way is the bookmark. Every post and Reel has one.

### Save it with one tap

1. Open the Reel or post with the recipe.
2. Tap the bookmark icon. On Reels it sits on the right side or under the share menu, and on feed posts it's below the photo on the right.
3. The post is now in your Saved list.

### Find your saved recipes later

Go to your profile, tap the menu in the top corner, and choose **Saved**. Everything you've bookmarked is there, newest first. Your saved list is private.

## How do you organize saved recipes into Instagram collections?

A single Saved list gets messy fast. Collections fix that.

### Create a collection

1. In your **Saved** list, tap the plus button to make a new collection.
2. Name it something you'll search for, like "Weeknight dinners," "Baking" or "Soups."
3. Pick saved posts to add to it.

You can also add a post to a collection as you save it. After tapping the bookmark, look for the option to save to a collection.

### Collection ideas that stay useful

- **By meal:** Breakfast, Lunches, Dinners, Desserts
- **By effort:** Under 30 minutes, Weekend projects
- **By event:** Holidays, Game day, Potluck
- **By status:** To try, Made it and loved it

## Why do saved Instagram recipes stop working?

A bookmark points to the creator's post. It doesn't copy the recipe. That causes a few problems.

- **Posts disappear.** If the creator deletes or archives the post, or makes their account private, your save can go with it.
- **Amounts are hidden.** Many Reels say the measurements out loud but never write them in the caption.
- **Cooking from a video is hard.** You pause, rewind and squint while the pan is heating. The screen dims at the worst moment.
- **Search doesn't help.** You can't search your saved posts by ingredient, so finding "that chicken thing" means scrolling.

## How do you turn an Instagram Reel into a written recipe?

The goal is a recipe with ingredients, amounts and steps you can follow without the video.

### Option 1: Copy it by hand

Watch the Reel, pause at each step, and write down what you see and hear. Check the caption and the comments, since creators often answer questions about amounts there. It works, but it takes 10 minutes or more per recipe.

### Option 2: Screenshot the caption

If the creator wrote the recipe in the caption, take a screenshot. It's quick, but you're stuck with a picture in your camera roll among hundreds of others.

### Option 3: Share it to a recipe app

Some recipe apps can read the Reel for you. With [Nasi](../../), you tap **Share** on the Reel, choose Nasi (or paste the link), and it watches the video, listens to the creator and reads the caption. You get ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, servings and cook time, with a link back to the creator.

| Method | Time | Keeps the recipe if the post is deleted | Shows amounts and steps | Searchable |

Most people use a mix: bookmark everything that looks good, then save the ones you actually plan to cook as written recipes.

## How do you keep Instagram recipes organized once you save them?

A few habits make a big difference.

- **Save with intent.** Ask yourself "will I make this in the next month?" If yes, save it as a recipe, not just a bookmark.
- **Name things the way you search.** "Chicken" and "Pasta" beat "Yum" and "Try this."
- **Clean up once a month.** Delete saves you'll never make. A smaller list gets used.
- **Plan from your saves.** Pick two or three saved recipes each week and put them on your meal plan.
