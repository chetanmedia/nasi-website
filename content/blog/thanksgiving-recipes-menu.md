**To plan a Thanksgiving menu, start with your guest count and your oven, then build around the turkey: 4 to 6 sides, 1 or 2 appetizers and 2 desserts.** Choose dishes that cook in different places (oven, stovetop, slow cooker, no-cook) and make as many ahead as you can. Then work backward from dinnertime with a written timeline, so the only thing left on Thursday is roasting and reheating.

Most Thanksgiving menu posts give you a pile of recipes. This one gives you a system. Below you'll find a sample menu, a three-week countdown, a day-of schedule and a simple way to turn it all into one grocery list.

## How do I start planning a Thanksgiving menu?

Answer four questions before you pick a single recipe. They decide everything else.

### How many people are coming?

Get a firm headcount, including kids. Ask about allergies and dietary needs now, not on Wednesday. If one guest is vegetarian, plan at least two sides that work as a filling plate.

### How much oven space do you have?

This is the question most hosts skip. A standard oven holds the turkey and maybe one small dish. Everything else has to cook before the turkey, while it rests, or somewhere else. Count your burners and any slow cooker or Instant Pot too.

### What time is dinner?

Pick an exact time, like 4:00 p.m. Every step on your timeline counts backward from it.

### Who is bringing what?

Guests love to help. Assign specific dishes rather than saying "bring whatever." Desserts, rolls, drinks and one side are easy to hand off.

## What should be on a Thanksgiving dinner menu?

A balanced menu has one of each role, not five versions of the same starch. Use this as a template.

| Role | Count | Examples | Where it cooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main | 1 | Roast turkey, turkey breast for small groups | Oven |
| Gravy | 1 | Pan gravy with a make-ahead base | Stovetop |
| Starch | 2 | Mashed potatoes, stuffing or dressing | Stovetop, oven |
| Sweet side | 1 | Sweet potato casserole, roasted squash | Oven |
| Green side | 1 or 2 | Green beans, Brussels sprouts, a crisp salad | Stovetop, no-cook |
| Bread | 1 | Dinner rolls or cornbread | Oven (reheat) |
| Sauce | 1 | Cranberry sauce | Stovetop, make ahead |
| Appetizers | 1 or 2 | Cheese board, crudités and dip | No-cook |
| Desserts | 2 | Pumpkin pie, apple or pecan pie | Oven, make ahead |

Notice the last column. A good menu spreads the work across the oven, the stovetop and the counter. If more than three dishes say "oven," swap one for a stovetop or slow cooker version. ### How do I keep the menu manageable?

Follow the same rule that works for any [weekly meal plan](../how-to-meal-plan-for-a-week/): mostly familiar dishes, one or two new ones. Thanksgiving is not the day to test three recipes you've never made. If you want to try something new, do a practice run the weekend before.

## What does a sample Thanksgiving menu look like?

Here's a classic menu for 10 to 12 people that fits one oven.

- **Appetizers:** cheese and cracker board, vegetables with a yogurt dip
- **Main:** 12- to 14-pound roast turkey
- **Gravy:** turkey gravy with a stock base made Tuesday
- **Starches:** mashed potatoes, herb bread stuffing baked in a dish
- **Sweet side:** sweet potato casserole
- **Greens:** garlicky green beans on the stovetop, a shaved Brussels sprouts salad
- **Bread:** store-bought or homemade dinner rolls
- **Sauce:** cranberry sauce
- **Desserts:** pumpkin pie and apple pie

Only the turkey needs the oven for hours. The stuffing, sweet potatoes and rolls go in while the turkey rests. Everything else lives on the stovetop or the counter.

## What is a good Thanksgiving cooking timeline?

Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday, November 26. Here's how to spread the work out so no single day feels like a marathon.

### Three weeks out (early November)

- Lock in the guest list and dinner time.
- Write the menu and assign dishes to guests.
- Order a fresh turkey, or buy a frozen one now while the selection is good.
- Check you have a roasting pan, a meat thermometer, serving dishes and enough chairs.

### Two weeks out

- Write the full grocery list from your recipes (more on this below).
- Buy shelf-stable items: canned pumpkin, broth, flour, sugar, spices, drinks.
- Make and freeze pie dough and dinner rolls if you're baking them.

### The weekend before

- Do a practice run of any new recipe.
- Clear space in the fridge. You'll need it.
- **Move a frozen turkey to the fridge.** Allow about 24 hours of thawing for every 4 to 5 pounds. A 16-pound bird needs about 4 days, so Sunday is the latest safe start.

### Tuesday

- Shop for fresh produce, dairy and herbs.
- Make cranberry sauce. It keeps well in the fridge.
- Make the gravy base with stock, so Thursday is just whisking in drippings.
- Cube and dry the bread for stuffing.

### Wednesday

- Bake the pies.
- Assemble the sweet potato casserole and stuffing, cover and refrigerate (bake them Thursday).
- Make mashed potatoes, or peel and keep potatoes in cold water.
- Trim green beans, wash salad greens, chop onions and celery.
- Set the table.

## What should I do on Thanksgiving Day, hour by hour?

This schedule assumes a 12- to 14-pound turkey and dinner at 4:00 p.m. Roasting times vary with your oven and the bird, so trust your thermometer, not the clock.

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 9:30 a.m. | Take the turkey out of the fridge. Pat it dry and season it. Preheat the oven to 325°F (163°C). |
| 10:30 a.m. | Turkey goes in the oven. |
| 12:30 p.m. | Take casseroles and stuffing out of the fridge so they bake evenly. |
| 1:30 p.m. | Start checking the turkey's temperature. A 12- to 14-pound unstuffed bird usually takes 3 to 3 3/4 hours. |
| 2:00 p.m. | Set out appetizers. |
| 2:30 p.m. | Once the turkey reads 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part of the thigh, wing and breast, take it out and let it rest at least 20 minutes. |
| 2:45 p.m. | Raise the oven heat. Bake the stuffing and sweet potatoes. |
| 3:15 p.m. | Reheat mashed potatoes on the stovetop. Cook the green beans. Finish the gravy with pan drippings. |
| 3:40 p.m. | Warm the rolls. Dress the salad. Carve the turkey. |
| 4:00 p.m. | Dinner. |

If the turkey finishes early, don't panic. Tent it loosely with foil. A large bird stays warm for a good while, and carving it just before serving matters more than timing it to the minute.

### What about food safety on the day?

- Thaw the turkey in the fridge, never on the counter.
- Cook stuffing in a separate dish. If you stuff the bird, the center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F.
- Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of serving, and eat them within 3 to 4 days.

## How do I make a Thanksgiving grocery list I can share?

Once your menu is set, go through each recipe and write down every ingredient with its amount. Combine duplicates as you go. If three recipes need butter, add the amounts and write one line.

Then group the list by store section: produce, meat, dairy, bakery, pantry, frozen. Share it with whoever is helping you shop, and split the list by section if you're dividing and conquering.

Two tips that save a second trip:

- **Scale before you shop.** If a recipe serves 6 and you have 12 guests, double it first, then write the list. When a recipe uses odd amounts, our guide to [how many tablespoons are in a cup](../how-many-tablespoons-in-a-cup/) makes the math easy.
- **Buy a little extra of the basics.** Butter, broth, onions and cream get used in everything. Keep the [ingredient substitution chart](../ingredient-substitution-chart/) handy for anything you still run out of.

## Your Thanksgiving plan, in short

1. Set the headcount, dinner time and who brings what.
2. Build the menu by role, and spread dishes across oven, stovetop and counter.
3. Write one grocery list from your scaled recipes.
4. Thaw the turkey in the fridge, starting the weekend before.
5. Make sauce, gravy base, pies and casseroles Tuesday and Wednesday.
6. On the day, roast, rest, reheat and carve.

Do the planning this week, and Thanksgiving Day becomes the easy part.
