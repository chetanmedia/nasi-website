**Thanksgiving dressing is bread, aromatics, broth and eggs, baked in its own dish until it's crisp on top and soft inside.** The classic version uses dried bread cubes, onion, celery, butter, sage and chicken or turkey broth. From there you can switch to cornbread, add sausage, apples or mushrooms, and make the whole thing a day ahead.

This guide covers the basic ratio that makes any dressing work, 15 variations grouped by style, and a make-ahead plan so the dressing is the easiest dish on your table.

## What is the difference between stuffing and dressing?

The difference is mostly about where it cooks.

- **Stuffing** goes inside the turkey.
- **Dressing** is baked in a separate pan.

In the South, "dressing" is the everyday word, and it often means cornbread dressing. Elsewhere, people say "stuffing" even when it never goes near the bird. Recipes use the two words interchangeably, so search for both.

Most cooks bake it in a pan now. The top gets crisp, the turkey roasts faster and more evenly, and you don't have to worry about undercooked stuffing in the center of the bird.

## What is the basic ratio for Thanksgiving dressing?

Learn one ratio and you can improvise any version. This makes a 9-by-13-inch pan, enough for 10 to 12 people.

| Ingredient | Amount | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Dried bread cubes | 1 pound (about 12 to 14 cups) | Stale or oven-dried bread soaks up broth without turning to mush |
| Butter | 1/2 cup (113 g) | Softens the vegetables and adds flavor |
| Onion and celery | 2 cups each, diced | The classic base |
| Fresh herbs | 2 tbsp sage, 1 tbsp thyme | Sage is the flavor most people expect |
| Broth | 2 1/2 to 3 cups (600 to 720 ml) | Add slowly until the bread is moist but not soggy |
| Eggs | 2, beaten | Hold it together like a soft bread pudding |

Bake it covered at 350°F (177°C) for 30 minutes, then uncovered for 15 to 25 minutes until the top is golden. The center should reach 165°F (74°C).

**Tip:** dry your bread a day or two early. Cut it into cubes and leave them out overnight, or toast them at 250°F (121°C) for about 45 minutes, stirring once.

## What are the best Thanksgiving dressing recipes to try?

Here are 15 variations, all built on the ratio above. Change the bread, add one or two mix-ins, and adjust the broth.

### Classic and herby

1. **Classic sage dressing.** White sandwich bread, onion, celery, sage and thyme. The one everyone remembers.
2. **Sourdough herb dressing.** Tangy sourdough holds its shape and gives a chewier texture.
3. **Brioche dressing.** Rich and tender. Use a little less butter and a little more broth.
4. **Herb and garlic butter dressing.** Lots of parsley, chives and roasted garlic folded in.

### Southern cornbread dressing

5. **Classic cornbread dressing.** Crumbled day-old cornbread mixed with some white bread for structure. Use more broth, because cornbread is thirstier.
6. **Cornbread and sausage dressing.** Brown 1 pound of pork sausage and add it with the vegetables.
7. **Cornbread dressing with pecans.** Toast the pecans first for a nutty crunch.
8. **Jalapeño cornbread dressing.** A little heat with diced jalapeño and sharp cheddar.

### Hearty and meaty

9. **Sausage and apple stuffing.** Sweet apple and savory sausage, the most popular pairing.
10. **Mushroom and leek dressing.** Earthy and deep. Cook the mushrooms until they're well browned before adding them.
11. **Bacon and caramelized onion dressing.** Smoky and sweet. Save some bacon fat to cook the onions.
12. **Oyster dressing.** A coastal tradition with briny oysters and their liquor in place of some of the broth.

### Fruit, nut and vegetarian

13. **Cranberry pecan dressing.** Dried cranberries for tartness, pecans for crunch.
14. **Wild rice and mushroom dressing.** Swap half the bread for cooked wild rice. Hearty and naturally lighter.
15. **Vegetarian dressing.** Use vegetable broth and plenty of mushrooms for a savory, meaty depth.

## How do I make dressing ahead of time?

Dressing is one of the best make-ahead dishes on the Thanksgiving table. Here's a simple timeline:

1. **Up to a month ahead:** bake the cornbread and freeze it if you're making cornbread dressing.
2. **Two days before:** cut and dry the bread cubes. Store them in a paper bag at room temperature.
3. **The day before:** cook the vegetables and any sausage or bacon. Let them cool, then mix everything with the bread, broth and eggs. Spread it in the buttered dish, cover and refrigerate.
4. **Thanksgiving Day:** take the dish out about 30 minutes before baking. Bake it while the turkey rests, adding 10 to 15 minutes because it starts cold.

Already-baked dressing also freezes well for up to 3 months. Thaw it in the fridge overnight and reheat it covered with a splash of broth.

## How do I keep my dressing from being soggy or dry?

Most dressing problems come from the bread or the broth.

### If it's soggy

- Use fully dried bread. Fresh bread turns to paste.
- Add broth a cup at a time. The bread should feel moist, not wet.
- Bake it uncovered for the last 15 to 25 minutes so the top can crisp.

### If it's dry

- Drizzle in another 1/4 to 1/2 cup of warm broth before baking.
- Keep it covered for the first half of the bake.
- Don't overbake. Pull it once the center reaches 165°F (74°C).

## Is it safe to cook stuffing inside the turkey?

It can be, but it takes care. The stuffing in the center of the bird has to reach 165°F (74°C), and by then the breast meat is often dry. If you stuff the turkey:

- Stuff it loosely, right before it goes in the oven, never the night before.
- Check the stuffing's temperature with a thermometer, not just the turkey's.
- Remove the stuffing from the bird as soon as it comes out of the oven.

Baking dressing in its own dish avoids all of this. You can find more detail in [USDA food safety guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/).

## How do I fit dressing into the Thanksgiving oven?

Dressing bakes best at 350°F to 375°F (177°C to 190°C), the same as most casseroles. The easy slot is the turkey's rest. Once the bird is out, it needs at least 20 minutes, so turn up the oven and bake the dressing alongside your other sides.
